Short answer: To keep pipes from freezing, keep the heat on, open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls, let faucets drip on the coldest nights, disconnect garden hoses, and insulate pipes in garages, attics and crawl spaces. If a pipe freezes, keep the faucet open and thaw it with gentle heat such as a hair dryer, never an open flame. If it bursts, shut off the main water valve right away.

A frozen pipe is a small problem; a burst pipe can flood a house in minutes. The steps below cost little and work in every climate, from a rare Texas freeze to a Minnesota January. Which pipes freeze first?

Pipes freeze where cold air reaches them and heat doesn't. The usual suspects:

  • Outdoor hose bibs, sprinkler lines and pool supply lines.
  • Pipes in unheated garages, attics, crawl spaces and basements.
  • Pipes in exterior walls, especially under kitchen and bathroom sinks.
  • Lines near gaps where cables, vents or dryer ducts pass through walls.
  • Water lines in vacant or seasonal homes.

How to keep pipes from freezing: before a cold snap

  1. Disconnect garden hoses and drain outdoor faucets. Shut off the inside valve that feeds them if you have one.
  2. Drain sprinkler and pool lines as the manufacturer or installer recommends.
  3. Insulate exposed pipes in garages, attics, crawl spaces and basements with foam pipe sleeves. Pay attention to elbows and joints.
  4. Seal air leaks near pipes with caulk or spray foam, and close crawl-space vents in winter.
  5. Add heat cable to pipes that freeze every year. Use a product rated for pipes and follow the instructions.
  6. Know your main shutoff. Find and test it now; see how to turn off the water to your house.

During a hard freeze

  • Keep the thermostat at the same temperature day and night, as the American Red Cross advises.
  • Open kitchen and bathroom cabinet doors so warm air reaches the pipes.
  • Let cold water drip from faucets served by exposed pipes.
  • Keep the garage door closed if water lines run through the garage.
  • If you leave, keep the heat on. The Red Cross says to set it no lower than 55°F.

How to unfreeze pipes safely

A frozen pipe often shows as a faucet that gives only a trickle or nothing at all. If you suspect one, act before it bursts.

  1. Open the faucet the pipe feeds. Flowing water helps melt the ice and relieves pressure.
  2. Find the frozen section. Check pipes along outside walls, in cabinets and in unheated spaces. Frost on the pipe is a clue.
  3. Apply gentle heat with a hair dryer, an electric heating pad or towels soaked in hot water. Start near the faucet and work back toward the frozen area.
  4. Never use an open flame such as a torch, propane heater or charcoal stove. The Red Cross warns against it; it's a fire risk and can damage pipe.
  5. Keep heating until full flow returns, then check other faucets; if one pipe froze, others may have too.
  6. Call a plumber if you can't find or reach the frozen spot, or if the thawed pipe leaks.

What to do if a pipe bursts

  1. Shut off the main water valve immediately.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas if you can reach the panel safely from a dry spot.
  3. Open faucets to drain remaining water from the lines.
  4. Move belongings and soak up water. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.
  5. Take photos and call your insurer.
  6. Call a plumber to repair the pipe and check for other damage.

Will insurance cover a burst pipe?

Often, yes, for sudden damage. The Insurance Information Institute says the standard HO-3 homeowners policy covers accidental discharge of water from a plumbing system. Insurers may deny claims if a home was left without heat or the damage built up over time. Read more in does homeowners insurance cover plumbing. This is general information, not insurance advice.

Long-term fixes for pipes that keep freezing

  • Reroute or insulate pipes that run in exterior walls or vented crawl spaces.
  • Install frost-free hose bibs, which shut off inside the heated part of the house.
  • Repipe with PEX in problem areas; it is flexible and handles freezing better than rigid pipe. See our guide to pipe types.
  • Add a smart leak or temperature sensor that alerts your phone.
  • Winterize seasonal homes by shutting off and draining the system.

Local note: Freezes hit hardest where pipes aren't built for them, such as attic lines in Texas and crawl spaces in Georgia and North Carolina. In Minnesota and Alaska, deep frost can freeze buried water lines, which usually needs a plumber with thawing equipment.

When should you call an emergency plumber?

  • A pipe has burst or is leaking after thawing.
  • You can't find or safely reach the frozen section.
  • Several faucets have no water.
  • Your buried water service line or meter is frozen.
  • The main shutoff valve won't close.
