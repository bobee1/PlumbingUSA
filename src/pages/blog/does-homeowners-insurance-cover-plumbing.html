Short answer: Does homeowners insurance cover plumbing? Usually yes for sudden, accidental water damage, such as a pipe that bursts or a water heater that suddenly leaks. The Insurance Information Institute says the standard HO-3 policy covers accidental discharge of water from a plumbing system. Policies usually don't pay to fix worn-out pipes or slow leaks you could have prevented. Sewer backups and floods need separate coverage.

After a leak, two bills arrive: one for the plumbing repair and one for the water damage. Insurance treats them very differently. This guide explains the usual rules so you know what to expect before you call your agent. PipeMend plumbers can fix the plumbing and document the cause, which helps with your claim. This is general information, not insurance advice; your policy and your agent decide what is covered.

What homeowners insurance usually covers

A standard policy is built around sudden and accidental events. For plumbing, that usually means:

  • Water damage from a burst pipe, including one that froze, if you kept the home heated.
  • Sudden leaks from a supply line, water heater, washing machine hose or dishwasher.
  • Overflow from a toilet, tub or appliance that malfunctions.
  • Tear-out and access costs on some policies, to reach the leak behind a wall or floor.
  • Your belongings damaged by covered water.
  • Additional living expenses if the home is unlivable during repairs.

What homeowners insurance usually doesn't cover

  • The broken part itself, such as a corroded pipe or a failed water heater.
  • Slow leaks and seepage. The Insurance Information Institute says water seepage is excluded under the HO-3 and is viewed as a maintenance issue.
  • Wear, rust, rot and neglect.
  • Sewer and drain backups, unless you add a water backup endorsement.
  • Flood water from outside. FEMA notes that standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage; flood insurance is a separate policy.
  • Mold, unless it results from a covered sudden leak and is within any mold limit.

Does insurance cover burst pipes from freezing?

Usually, if you took reasonable care. Insurers may deny a claim if a home was left without heat or the water wasn't shut off and drained while vacant. Read how to keep pipes from freezing for the steps that both prevent damage and protect your claim.

Does insurance cover a leaking water heater?

A sudden leak that damages floors and walls is often covered, but the heater itself is usually not, because it failed from age. See what to do when a water heater leaks and how long water heaters last.

Endorsements worth asking about

  • Water backup coverage for sewer and drain backups and sump pump overflow.
  • Service line coverage for buried water and sewer lines on your property.
  • Equipment breakdown coverage for some mechanical failures.
  • Higher mold limits in humid climates.
  • Flood insurance through FEMA's National Flood Insurance Program or a private insurer.

Does a home warranty cover plumbing?

A home warranty is a service contract, not insurance. It may pay to repair or replace covered systems that fail from normal wear, such as a water heater or interior pipes, usually for a service fee per visit. It usually doesn't pay for water damage, and many contracts limit coverage for pre-existing problems, sewer lines and access costs. Insurance covers damage from sudden events; a warranty covers some breakdowns. Read the contract's exclusions carefully.

How to file a plumbing claim

  1. Stop the water. See how to turn off water to your house.
  2. Document everything with photos and video before cleanup.
  3. Prevent more damage. Insurers expect reasonable steps. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.
  4. Call a plumber and ask for a written note of the cause.
  5. Call your insurer and get a claim number.
  6. Keep receipts and damaged parts until the adjuster has seen them.

How to keep plumbing claims from happening

  • Check under sinks and behind toilets every few months.
  • Replace washing machine hoses with braided steel hoses.
  • Insulate pipes in unheated spaces.
  • Install leak sensors or an automatic shutoff valve.
  • Replace aging water heaters before they fail.

Who pays when a leak comes from a neighbor's unit?

In condos and apartments, water often comes from the unit above. In general, the policy for the unit where the damage happens responds first, and the insurers may later sort out who was at fault. Condo owners usually need an HO-6 policy for interior finishes and belongings, while the association's master policy covers the building structure. Renters need their own policy for belongings. Read your association documents and your policy, and report the leak to the building manager right away.

Keep records that help your claim

Insurers look closely at whether damage was sudden or built up over time. Dated photos of your plumbing, receipts for water heater service and pipe insulation, and a plumber's written cause of the leak all help show that you maintained the home. Save them in one folder you can find quickly.

Local note: Basement sewer backups are common in Illinois and Michigan, which makes water backup coverage worth asking about there. Slab leaks are a frequent claim question in Texas and Florida, and flood insurance matters in Louisiana.

When should you call a plumber after a leak?

  • Water is still running or you can't find the source.
  • A pipe has burst or a water heater is leaking.
  • Sewage has backed up into the home.
  • You need a written cause for your insurance claim.
