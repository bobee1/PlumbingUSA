Short answer: If your water heater is leaking, shut off the cold water valve on top, turn off the gas or the breaker, and find where the water is coming from. A water heater leaking from the bottom of the tank itself usually means the tank has rusted through and must be replaced. Leaks from fittings, the drain valve or the T&P relief valve are often repairable. If you smell gas, leave first and call 911.

Finding a puddle under the water heater is stressful, especially when you don't know whether it's a drip or the start of a flood. What to do first when your water heater is leaking

  1. Stay safe. If you smell gas, leave the building on foot and call 911 once you are a safe distance away. PHMSA, the federal pipeline safety agency, also says to avoid anything that could make a spark.
  2. Turn off the fuel or power. On a gas heater, set the control knob to off. On an electric heater, switch off its breaker.
  3. Close the cold water supply. Turn the valve on the cold inlet pipe at the top of the heater clockwise. If it won't close, shut off the main water valve to the house.
  4. Contain the water. Use towels, a wet/dry vacuum and buckets. Move boxes and anything valuable away.
  5. Take photos. Photograph the heater, the label and any damage for your records and your insurer.
  6. Dry the area. The EPA advises drying wet building materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.

Why is my water heater leaking?

Most leaks come from one of six places. The location of the water is your best clue.

Water heater leaking from the bottom

A water heater leaking from the bottom is the most serious case. First rule out the drain valve: dry it with a towel and watch whether drips form at its spout or stem. Next, check whether water is running down from the top and collecting at the base. If the drain valve is dry and the top is dry, the steel tank has most likely rusted through. A tank leak can't be patched; the heater needs to be replaced.

Sediment speeds this up. Minerals settle on the tank bottom, trap heat and let corrosion work faster. That is why the Department of Energy suggests draining a quart of water from the tank every three months.

Water heater leaking from the top

Leaks at the top are usually good news, because they are often fixable. Check the flexible connectors on the cold and hot lines, the threaded nipples where they enter the tank, and the anode rod port. A plumber can replace connectors, nipples and dielectric unions without replacing the heater.

Is the T&P relief valve leaking?

The temperature and pressure (T&P) relief valve is a safety part. It opens to release water if the tank gets too hot or the pressure climbs too high. A valve that drips or discharges is warning you about something:

  • High water pressure from the street or a failed pressure reducing valve.
  • Thermal expansion in a closed system with no expansion tank.
  • Water set too hot. The CPSC urges a setting of 120°F to prevent scalds.
  • A worn valve that no longer seals.

Never cap or plug the discharge pipe. That disables the safety feature.

What about a leaking tankless water heater?

Tankless units can leak at their water connections, pressure relief valve or heat exchanger. Scale from hard water and freezing in unheated spaces are common causes. Shut off the water and power or gas, and have the unit checked. If you are weighing a new heater anyway, compare options in our tankless vs. tank water heater guide.

How to prevent water heater leaks

  • Drain a little water from the tank every few months to clear sediment.
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years.
  • Test the T&P relief valve once a year.
  • Keep the temperature near 120°F.
  • Install a drain pan and a leak alarm under heaters in attics, closets or finished basements.
  • Ask about an expansion tank if your home has a pressure regulator or check valve.

Local note: Hard water shortens tank life in Arizona, Nevada and Texas, so flushing matters more there. In California, a replacement heater must be strapped for earthquakes, and basements in Minnesota hide slow leaks until they spread.

When should you call a plumber?

  • Water is coming from the tank body.
  • The T&P relief valve keeps dripping or discharging.
  • You smell gas or see scorch marks near the burner.
  • The cold water valve won't close.
  • The heater is 10 years old or more and leaking anywhere.
