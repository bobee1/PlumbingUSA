Quick answer: Garbage disposal repair clears jams, resets tripped motors, fixes leaks at the sink flange and drain connections, and clears the sink drain behind the unit. When a disposal leaks from its body, rusts through or burns out, a plumber replaces it with a new unit sized for your use. Most disposal problems start with grease or hard, stringy food going down the drain.

What garbage disposal service includes

  • Jam clearing and flywheel freeing.
  • Reset and power checks: the reset button, switch, outlet and breaker.
  • Leak repair at the sink flange, dishwasher inlet and drain elbow.
  • Sink drain clearing through the trap and branch line.
  • Replacement with a new unit and a new mounting assembly.
  • Dishwasher connection and air gap or high loop where required.
  • Haul-away of the old unit.

The garbage disposal repair process

  1. Cut power. The disposal is unplugged or the breaker is switched off before anyone reaches in.
  2. Diagnose. The plumber checks power, the reset, the flywheel, the seals and the drain.
  3. Clear or repair. Jams are freed and leaking connections are resealed.
  4. Clear the drain. If water still backs up, the trap and branch drain are cleared.
  5. Quote replacement if needed. A unit leaking from its body or with a failing motor gets a written replacement price.
  6. Test. The disposal runs with water, and the dishwasher drains through it without leaks.

Grease: the disposal's worst enemy

Grease goes down warm and liquid, then cools and sticks to the inside of pipes, catching food scraps as it builds. The EPA's fact sheet on fats, oils and grease says grease is the most common cause of reported sewer blockages. The EPA also lists grease among the main causes of sewer overflows and backups. A disposal grinds food; it doesn't make grease safe to pour. Collect cooking grease in a can and throw it away. For grease clogs further down the line, see hydro jetting.

Choosing a new garbage disposal

  • Motor size: 1/3 to 1/2 horsepower suits light use; 3/4 to 1 horsepower handles heavier use and tougher scraps.
  • Feed type: continuous feed runs with a wall switch; batch feed runs only with the lid in place.
  • Septic systems: ask about models made for septic use, and use the disposal sparingly.
  • Noise: insulated models are quieter.
  • Space: the plumber checks room under the sink and drain height.

What affects the cost of disposal repair

  • Repair or replacement.
  • Motor size and brand of a new unit.
  • Electrical work, such as a new switch or outlet.
  • Drain clearing behind the unit.
  • Dishwasher connection changes.

DIY or call a professional?

With the power off, you can press the reset button and use the hex wrench that came with the unit to free a jam. Never put your hand in the disposal. Call a plumber for leaks, electrical problems, repeat clogs or replacement. The EPA's WaterSense program notes that small leaks add up, and a slow cabinet leak also damages the cabinet floor. For a backed-up kitchen sink, see how to unclog a drain without harsh chemicals.

Keep your disposal running

  • Run cold water before, during and after grinding.
  • Feed scraps a little at a time.
  • Keep grease, coffee grounds, eggshells and fibrous peels out.
  • Grind a few ice cubes now and then to clean the chamber.
