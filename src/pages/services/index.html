Plumbing companies near me: what to ask before you hire

Whoever you call, a few questions protect you from surprise bills and poor work.

  • Is the plumber licensed for this work in my state and city? Can I see the license number?
  • Will I get a written estimate before work starts, and will you explain any change in scope first?
  • Does this job need a permit and inspection? Who pulls it?
  • Is gas work done by a plumber allowed to do gas work here?
  • What warranty comes with the parts and the labor?
  • Can you show me the problem, with photos or a camera video, before you fix it?

Repair or replace?

Many plumbing calls end with that choice. A single leaking joint or worn part is usually repaired. Replacement makes more sense when the same pipe or fixture keeps failing, when parts are no longer made, or when a water heater tank itself is leaking. The plumber will explain both options and their costs, so you can decide.
