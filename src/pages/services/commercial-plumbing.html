Quick answer: Commercial plumbing covers the bigger, busier systems in shops, offices, restaurants and rental buildings. Common jobs are clearing main drains and grease lines, fixing restroom fixtures, and servicing large water heaters. Backflow tests and gas lines for kitchen gear are common too. Work is often set around business hours, and it is written up for inspectors, landlords and insurers.

What commercial plumbing includes

  • Drain and sewer service: main lines, floor drains, kitchen lines and camera inspection.
  • Hydro jetting of grease-laden kitchen lines.
  • Grease interceptor and trap plumbing repairs.
  • Restrooms: flush valves, sensor faucets, urinals and ADA-height fixtures.
  • Commercial water heaters and tankless systems.
  • Backflow testing, repair and installation.
  • Gas lines for kitchen ranges, fryers and rooftop units.
  • Leak detection and repiping in occupied buildings.
  • Preventive maintenance plans with written reports.

How commercial service works

  1. Call and describe the problem, including access instructions and business hours.
  2. Schedule around your hours, with after-hours or weekend work when needed.
  3. Diagnose with cameras, locators and pressure tests, and get a written estimate.
  4. Repair with permits and inspections where required.
  5. Document. You receive a written report with what was found and what was done.
  6. Plan ahead. Recurring issues can go on a maintenance schedule.

Grease control for food service

The EPA says grease is the most common cause of reported sewer blockages. Its fact sheet notes that grease from restaurants is a big part of the problem. Many sewer agencies require a grease trap or interceptor. They also ask for pumping records. A plumber keeps the kitchen line clear with regular hydro jetting. Plumbers also repair the inlet and outlet piping on the interceptor. The EPA also lists grease among the main causes of sewer overflows, which can bring fines from local authorities.

Water-efficient commercial restrooms

The EPA estimates there are nearly 27 million flushometer-valve toilets installed in the United States. It says about a quarter of the older ones use 3 to 7 gallons per flush, far above the 1.6-gallon federal standard. WaterSense labeled flushometer-valve toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush or less. New flush valves and fixtures can lower water bills in busy buildings. See toilet repair and faucet repair.

Backflow and code compliance

Commercial water connections, fire sprinkler lines and irrigation systems usually need approved backflow preventers and regular tests. An EPA fact sheet describes cases where unprotected cross-connections let irrigation water and firefighting foam flow back into public water. See backflow testing for test and repair details.

Property managers and multi-site businesses

If you manage several buildings or locations, one phone number for every site makes service simpler. PipeMend can keep notes on each property's shutoff locations, backflow assemblies, water heaters and past repairs. That way the next plumber starts with history, not guesswork. Tenants can be told when water will be off, and reports can go straight to the owner or the management office.

What affects the cost of commercial plumbing

  • Building size and pipe size.
  • After-hours or weekend scheduling.
  • Access in occupied or finished spaces.
  • Permits, inspections and code updates.
  • One-time repair or an ongoing maintenance plan.

DIY or call a professional?

Staff can plunge a toilet and keep grease out of drains. Most commercial work needs a licensed plumber, and much of it needs permits. That includes gas lines, backflow assemblies, water heaters and shared drain stacks. Using a professional also gives you the paperwork landlords, insurers and inspectors ask for.

Prevent business interruptions

  • Train kitchen staff to scrape plates and keep grease out of sinks.
  • Put drain jetting and backflow tests on a calendar.
  • Label shutoff valves so staff can find them in an emergency.
  • Fix running toilets and dripping faucets quickly in busy restrooms.
