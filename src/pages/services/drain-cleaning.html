Quick answer: Drain cleaning clears clogs from sinks, tubs, showers, floor drains and the main line using hand or machine augers, and, for heavy buildup, a water jetter. A good plumber also finds the cause. The EPA's Report to Congress found that grease is the most common cause of reported sewer blockages.

What drain cleaning includes

  • Diagnosis: which fixtures are slow, where the clog is likely to be, and the best access point.
  • Trap cleaning for sinks and tubs, including removal of hair, soap and debris.
  • Drum or cable augering through branch lines and the main drain.
  • Cleanout access on the main line, so the house is not opened up.
  • Camera inspection when clogs return or more than one drain is slow.
  • Flow test of every affected fixture after the clog is cleared.
  • Advice on prevention based on what came out of the pipe.

The drain cleaning process, step by step

  1. Ask and look. The plumber asks which drains are slow, runs water and listens for gurgling to judge where the clog sits.
  2. Protect the area. Floors and finishes are covered, and the fixture is drained down.
  3. Choose the access. A trap, an overflow, a toilet flange or an outdoor cleanout gives the most direct route to the clog.
  4. Clear the line. An auger with the right head, such as a spade, cutter or retrieval head, breaks up or pulls out the blockage.
  5. Check the result. Water is run at full flow for several minutes. On main lines, a camera shows whether roots, sags or breaks remain.
  6. Explain the cause. You see what came out and get advice to stop it happening again.

Signs of a main drain clog

  • Two or more fixtures drain slowly at the same time.
  • The toilet bubbles or the tub drain backs up when the washing machine empties.
  • Water rises in a basement floor drain or the lowest shower.
  • You hear gurgling in drains after flushing.
  • A sewage smell comes from floor drains.

A main line clog affects the whole house, so stop running water and call. Pushing more water into a blocked main can flood the lowest drain.

Snake, jetter or camera: which tool when

A drain snake (auger) is a coiled steel cable that bores through a blockage. It is the right first tool for most sink, tub and toilet clogs. A sewer camera is a waterproof camera on a push cable that shows the inside of the pipe. It finds roots, breaks, sags called "bellies," and offset joints. Hydro jetting sprays water at high pressure to scour grease, scale and roots off the whole pipe wall. Jetting is best for main lines with recurring buildup, once the camera shows the pipe can take the pressure.

Why grease and wipes cause so many clogs

Fats, oils and grease go down the drain warm and liquid, then cool and stick to pipe walls. Each layer catches more food and soap. The EPA lists grease and some products labeled "flushable," such as baby wipes and facial wipes, among the materials that block sewers. Wipes do not break apart like toilet paper, and they snag on rough spots and roots. Keeping both out of your drains prevents most kitchen and toilet clogs.

What affects the cost of drain cleaning

  • Which drain is blocked, and how far from an access point the clog sits.
  • Whether the toilet has to be pulled or a cleanout has to be found or added.
  • Main line versus a single fixture.
  • Camera inspection or hydro jetting.
  • Time of day, if the backup cannot wait.

DIY or call a professional?

A plunger, a plastic drain stick or cleaning a sink trap can clear many single-fixture clogs. Call a plumber when the clog comes back within weeks, when more than one drain is slow, when water backs up into another fixture, or when you have already used a chemical cleaner. Rented machine augers can tear up old pipes and injure hands if used the wrong way.

How to keep drains clear

  • Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing them.
  • Use a hair catcher in tubs and showers, and empty it weekly.
  • Flush only toilet paper.
  • Run hot water for a few seconds after using the garbage disposal.
  • Pour water into rarely used floor drains every few weeks to keep the trap full.
