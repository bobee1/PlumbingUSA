Quick answer: Water heater repair fixes the parts that fail most: thermocouples, igniters, gas controls, heating elements, thermostats, drain valves and T&P relief valves. Most repairs take one visit. If the tank itself leaks, replacement is the only fix. The Department of Energy says most tank heaters last 10 to 15 years.

What water heater repair includes

  • Safety check of gas supply, venting, the flue and the T&P (temperature and pressure) relief valve.
  • Diagnosis of gas burners, pilots, igniters and flame sensors, or electric elements and thermostats.
  • Part replacement: thermocouple, thermopile, gas control valve, igniter, heating element, thermostat, drain valve, dip tube or anode rod.
  • Leak repair at fittings, unions, the drain valve and the relief valve line.
  • Sediment flushing when buildup is causing noise or slow recovery.
  • Temperature setting checked and set to a safe level.
  • Repair or replace advice with the cost of each option.

The water heater repair process

  1. Make it safe. The plumber checks for gas smell, scorch marks and proper venting, and shuts off fuel or power before opening panels.
  2. Read the label. Age, fuel type, capacity and model point to likely failures and part availability.
  3. Test the parts. On gas units: pilot, thermocouple output, igniter and gas control. On electric units: voltage, element resistance and thermostats.
  4. Find any leak. Fittings, valves and the tank bottom are checked to see whether the tank itself has failed.
  5. Quote the fix. You get a written estimate for repair, and for replacement when the tank is old or leaking.
  6. Repair and test. The part is replaced, the heater relit or powered, and the outlet temperature and relief valve checked.

When repair makes sense, and when to replace

Repair is usually the better value when the heater is well inside its expected life and a single part has failed. Thermocouples, elements, igniters and valves are common, low-cost parts. Replacement usually makes more sense when:

  • The tank body is leaking or rusted through.
  • The heater is near or past 10 to 15 years old and needs a costly part, such as a gas control.
  • Hot water stays rusty after the anode is replaced.
  • The unit is too small for the household and runs out every day.

If replacement is the better choice, see water heater installation or tankless water heaters.

The T&P relief valve: a safety part, not a nuisance

Every tank heater has a temperature and pressure relief valve near the top. It opens if the tank gets too hot or the pressure climbs too high. A relief valve that drips or discharges is telling you something: water pressure may be too high, a thermal expansion tank may be needed, or the thermostat may be failing. Never cap or plug the discharge pipe. A plumber can test the valve and find the cause.

Leaking water heater: what to do now

  1. Shut off the cold supply at the valve on the pipe going into the top of the heater.
  2. Turn off the fuel or power. Set the gas control to "off" or switch off the breaker.
  3. Dry the area. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.
  4. Call a plumber. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and the gas company first.

Set the temperature safely

The Department of Energy says 120°F is a temperature considered safe for most people, and it saves energy. The Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) also urges setting heaters to 120°F. It warns that 140°F water can cause third-degree burns in six seconds. If you lower the setting, a plumber can check that your dishwasher and fixtures still get the hot water they need.

What affects the cost of water heater repair

  • Gas or electric, and the brand and model.
  • Which part failed, and whether it is in stock.
  • Access: attic, closet, crawl space or garage.
  • Code updates needed on venting, gas lines or the relief valve line.
  • Time of day for an emergency call.

DIY or call a professional?

You can relight a pilot by following the label on the heater, reset an electric heater's breaker, and drain a few gallons from the drain valve. Call a plumber for anything involving the gas control, burner, venting, wiring or the relief valve, or if the heater leaks. Gas appliances that are not burning right can produce carbon monoxide.

Maintenance that prevents repairs

  • The Department of Energy suggests draining a quart of water from the tank every 3 months to remove sediment. Follow the manufacturer's directions.
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years, more often with hard or softened water.
  • Test the T&P relief valve once a year.
  • Keep the area around a gas heater clear of storage.
