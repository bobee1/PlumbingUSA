Quick answer: A slab leak is a leak in a water pipe under a concrete floor. Repair starts with pinpoint detection, using pressure tests, acoustic gear and thermal cameras. The fix is then one of three: open the slab and spot-repair the pipe, reroute the line above ground, or repipe the house when the old pipe keeps failing.

Where slab leaks happen

What slab leak repair includes

  • Confirmation with meter and pressure tests on hot and cold lines.
  • Pinpoint detection with acoustic listening, thermal imaging and moisture readings.
  • Floor protection and careful removal of flooring at the leak spot.
  • Slab opening with a saw and jackhammer, limited to the marked area.
  • Pipe repair with copper or PEX fittings, then a pressure test.
  • Backfill and concrete patch to restore the slab.
  • Reroute or repipe options priced side by side.

The slab leak repair process

  1. Isolate. The plumber tests hot and cold lines separately to see which one leaks.
  2. Pinpoint. Acoustic and thermal tools narrow the leak to a small spot on the floor.
  3. Quote options. You get prices for spot repair and reroute, and repipe if the pipe is worn.
  4. Repair. The chosen repair is made, and the line is pressure tested before closing.
  5. Restore. The slab is patched. Flooring is usually replaced by a flooring contractor.
  6. Check for damage. Moisture readings show whether drying or restoration is needed.

Why slab leaks happen

  • Abrasion: pipes rub against concrete, gravel or each other as they expand and contract, especially hot lines.
  • Corrosion: water chemistry and soil conditions wear copper from the inside or outside.
  • Soil movement: expansive clay soils, common in Texas and parts of the West, shift the slab and stress pipes.
  • Installation defects: kinked pipe or poor joints from the original build.
  • High water pressure that strains pipes and joints.

Signs of a slab leak

  • Warm or hot spots on the floor.
  • Sound of running water with all fixtures off.
  • Water meter moving when no water is in use.
  • Damp carpet, cupping wood floors or loose tiles.
  • New cracks in the floor or walls.
  • Higher water or gas bills from a water heater that runs nonstop.

The EPA's WaterSense program suggests reading your meter, using no water for two hours, then reading it again. If the reading changed, you likely have a leak. Our leak detection service covers the next step.

What affects the cost of slab leak repair

  • How deep the pipe is and how thick the slab is.
  • Flooring over the leak, such as tile, hardwood or carpet.
  • Repair method: spot repair, reroute or repipe.
  • Number of leaks and the pipe's overall condition.
  • Water damage repairs and drying.

DIY or call a professional?

You can do the meter test and shut off the main valve if water appears. Finding and repairing a slab leak needs specialized tools, concrete work and pressure testing, so call a licensed plumber. Breaking a slab in the wrong spot is costly.

Limit the damage

  • Shut off the main water valve if water is surfacing.
  • Pull up wet rugs and move furniture.
  • Dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, as the EPA advises, to prevent mold.
  • Photograph the damage and call your insurer.
