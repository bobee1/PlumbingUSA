Quick answer: Tankless water heater installation replaces a storage tank with a wall-mounted unit that heats water as it flows. The Department of Energy says tankless units can be 24 to 34 percent more efficient than a tank in homes using 41 gallons or less of hot water a day, and they can last more than 20 years. Correct sizing, gas line, venting and descaling make or break the results.

Tank vs. tankless: how they work

What tankless water heater service includes

  • Sizing by peak flow in gallons per minute and the temperature rise your climate needs.
  • Gas line check and upsizing when the unit needs more gas than the old tank.
  • Venting: new stainless or PVC venting to the manufacturer's specs.
  • Electrical: a condensate pump or outlet for gas units; dedicated circuits for electric units.
  • Isolation valves so the unit can be flushed without draining the house.
  • Descaling with a pump and descaler, plus inlet filter cleaning.
  • Repair of error codes, ignition failures, flame sensors, flow sensors and fans.

Sizing a tankless water heater

Tankless sizing has two numbers. The first is flow: how many gallons per minute you need when the most fixtures run at once, such as two showers and a sink. The second is temperature rise: how much the unit must heat incoming water. The Department of Energy's Building America program says this depends on your area's winter cold water temperature and your hot water setting. Water in northern states can arrive much colder than in the South, so the same unit delivers fewer gallons per minute in Minnesota than in Florida. The DOE says high-efficiency tankless units can provide hot water at 2 to 5 gallons per minute.

The tankless installation process

  1. Assess. The plumber measures gas line size and length, checks the electrical panel and plans the vent route.
  2. Size and quote. You get a written estimate with the unit, gas, venting, electrical and permit work spelled out.
  3. Remove the tank. The old heater is drained, disconnected and hauled away.
  4. Mount the unit. The DOE notes that units should be installed level with rust- and corrosion-proof hardware.
  5. Connect. Water lines with isolation valves, the gas line, venting, the condensate drain and power are installed.
  6. Start up and test. Gas pressure, combustion, flow and outlet temperature are checked, and the setpoint is set.

Venting in cold climates

Gas tankless units vent hot exhaust and, in condensing models, produce acidic condensate that must be drained. The DOE says cold-climate units should be sealed-combustion, direct-vent or power-vented. It also says vents should sit above the expected snow line, with the intake far enough from the exhaust to prevent frost buildup. Getting this wrong causes shutdowns in winter.

Is tankless right for your home?

Tankless works well for small spaces, for long showers, and for homes that keep a heater for many years. The DOE says savings are largest in homes using 41 gallons or less a day, and smaller (8 to 14 percent) in homes using around 86 gallons a day. A tank may still make more sense when upfront cost matters most, or when the gas line or electrical service would need a costly upgrade. Compare both with tank water heater installation.

What affects the cost of a tankless water heater

  • Gas or electric, and the unit's size and brand.
  • Gas line upsizing and new venting.
  • Electrical work: a new circuit, or a panel upgrade for electric units.
  • Recirculation pumps or a water softener ahead of the unit.
  • Permit fees and removal of the old tank.

DIY or call a professional?

You can clean the inlet filter and follow the owner's manual for descaling if the unit has isolation valves. Installation, gas work, venting and electrical hookups need a licensed plumber and usually a permit. Many manufacturers also require professional installation for the warranty.

Keep it safe and efficient

  • Set the temperature around 120°F. The CPSC urges this setting to prevent scalds.
  • Descale on the schedule in your owner's manual, more often with hard water.
  • Keep vent terminals clear of snow, leaves and nests.
