Smell gas? Leave the building now. Don't flip switches, use your phone indoors or start a car. Once you're a safe distance away, call 911 and your gas company. Call a plumber for repairs only after they say it's safe.

Quick answer: Gas line repair fixes leaks and damaged piping on your side of the gas meter, then pressure tests the system before the gas is turned back on. Gas line installation runs new piping to appliances such as ranges, dryers, water heaters, fireplaces, grills and standby generators. Gas work usually needs a permit and inspection, and it must be done by a qualified, licensed professional.

What gas line service includes

  • Leak location with electronic gas detectors and leak solution at fittings.
  • Pressure testing of the piping system.
  • Repair or replacement of leaking fittings, valves and damaged pipe.
  • New gas lines for appliances, outdoor kitchens, pool heaters and generators.
  • Appliance shutoff valves and sediment traps.
  • Load and sizing check so every appliance gets enough gas.
  • CSST bonding checks where required.
  • Permits, inspection and coordination with the gas utility for turn-on.

The gas line repair process

  1. Make it safe. Gas is shut off at the meter or the appliance valve, and the area is ventilated.
  2. Find the leak. Detectors and leak solution pinpoint the fitting or section.
  3. Quote. You get a written estimate, including permit and inspection if needed.
  4. Repair. Fittings are remade or pipe is replaced with approved materials.
  5. Pressure test. The system holds test pressure for the required time with no drop.
  6. Restore service. Appliances are relit and checked, with the utility involved where required.

Running a new gas line

Adding a gas range, dryer, fireplace, grill or generator means more demand on your piping. The plumber totals the BTU ratings of all your appliances. Then the pipe size is matched to the length of the run and your gas pressure, following the fuel gas code your area uses, such as the International Fuel Gas Code or NFPA 54. Undersized lines starve appliances, causing poor burner performance or shutdowns. Each appliance gets its own shutoff valve.

Carbon monoxide and gas appliances

Gas appliances that aren't installed or vented right can produce carbon monoxide (CO), which you can't see or smell. The CPSC says most appliances should be installed by qualified professionals. It recommends CO alarms on every level of the home and outside sleeping areas, with battery backup. Interconnected alarms are best, so when one sounds, they all sound. What affects the cost of gas line work

  • Repair or a new line, and the length of the run.
  • Pipe material: black iron, CSST or underground polyethylene.
  • Access through walls, floors, crawl spaces or yards.
  • Upsizing if the existing system is too small for the added load.
  • Permit, inspection and pressure test requirements.

DIY or call a professional?

Gas piping is not a DIY job. A small mistake can cause a fire, an explosion or carbon monoxide exposure, and most places require a licensed professional and a permit. What you can do: know where your meter shutoff is, keep appliance areas clear, and install CO alarms.

Gas safety at home

  • Learn what natural gas smells like, so you recognize it.
  • Call 811 before digging, so buried gas lines are marked.
  • Have gas appliances and vents checked once a year.
  • Keep the area around the meter clear and accessible.
