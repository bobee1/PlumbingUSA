Who writes our pages

Guides and service pages carry the byline {{editorial_byline}}. The editorial team picks topics from real questions people search for about plumbing, researches each one, writes a draft, checks every claim against the listed sources and reviews the page before it is published. {{owner}}, owner of {{brand}}, is accountable for everything on the site.

We use software, including AI writing tools, to help with research and first drafts. A person checks each page against its sources before it goes live, and no tool decides which facts a page contains.

Sources we rely on

We favor primary and official sources, in this order:

  1. Federal agencies and national safety groups: the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), including its WaterSense and drinking water programs; the U.S. Department of Energy; the Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC); the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC); the Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration (PHMSA); and the American Red Cross.
  2. State and local government: state plumbing and contractor licensing boards, state statutes, public utility commissions, water and sewer utilities, and city or county building departments.
  3. Model codes and standards: cited by name and edition, such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and the National Fuel Gas Code (NFPA 54).
  4. Manufacturer instructions: for product-specific steps, such as how a particular water heater is drained or relit.
  5. Company sources: used for a single figure only, and credited by name in the sentence.

Every source is opened and read before we cite it. A search result snippet never counts as a source.

How facts are checked

  • Each number, deadline and rule on a page links back to a source listed on that page.
  • Laws and codes are cited with section numbers, editions and effective dates, and confirmed to be in force on the day the page is published.
  • Safety topics, such as gas leaks, carbon monoxide from water heaters and sewage exposure, follow official agency guidance only. We tell readers to call 911 or a doctor when that is the right step.
  • Insurance and legal topics carry a "general information, not advice" note and remind readers that policies differ.

Things we never do

  • Publish fake reviews, ratings, testimonials or before-and-after stories.
  • Invent statistics or prices. Cost content sticks to the factors that move a price.
  • Claim credentials, awards or partnerships we do not have.
  • Change what a guide says because a manufacturer or company paid us.
  • Pass off stock photos as pictures of our own jobs.

When pages are reviewed

Each service page and guide shows its last review date. Pages are reviewed at least every 12 months, and sooner when a law, code edition or safety rule changes, or when a hard freeze or a new product standard shows us something readers need.

How we earn money

{{brand}} is paid for plumbing jobs that begin with a call or form request, which is why our phone number appears on every page. Payment does not change the facts in our guides. If a problem is something you can safely fix yourself, such as a loose faucet aerator or a clogged sink trap, our guides say so and explain how.

Corrections

Found an error, an outdated rule or a broken link? Tell us through the contact page or by calling {{phone}}. Every report is reviewed. Material fixes are made promptly, and the page's review date is updated.
