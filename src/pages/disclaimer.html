General information only

The guides, checklists and answers on {{domain}} describe plumbing problems, repairs, water heaters, drains, sewer lines and related insurance questions in general terms. They are not legal, insurance, medical, engineering or code-compliance advice, and reading them does not create a professional relationship. Plumbing codes and laws vary by state, county and city, and no two homes or insurance policies are the same.

  • Coverage questions: read your policy and ask your insurance agent or company.
  • Legal questions, such as a dispute with a landlord, a contractor or a home seller: ask a lawyer licensed in your state.
  • Health questions, such as illness after contact with sewage or concerns about lead in drinking water: ask a doctor or your local health department.
  • Code and structural questions: ask your local building department or a licensed plumber or engineer.

Do-it-yourself work and safety

Some guides explain small tasks a homeowner can try, such as closing a shutoff valve or clearing a sink trap. Any do-it-yourself work is at your own risk. Stop and call a licensed plumber if a task involves gas lines or gas appliances, water heater relief valves or burners, electrical parts near water, sewage, or any work your local code says must be done by a licensed person or under a permit. If you smell gas, leave the building first and call 911 and your gas company from a safe distance.

Prices

Licensing, permits and inspections

Most states and many cities license plumbers, and some require a separate license or permit for gas piping, backflow testing or water heater installation. Many repairs and almost all replacements of water heaters, sewer lines and gas lines need a permit and an inspection. Before work starts, you may ask any plumber for their license number and check it with your state or local licensing board.
