Plumbing services for every part of your home's water system

Every house has three systems that work together. Supply pipes bring clean water in under pressure. A water heater warms part of it. A drain-waste-vent system uses gravity to carry used water out to the sewer or a septic tank.

  1. Main shutoff valve. Where the water service enters the house. Knowing where it is turns a flood into a puddle.
  2. Water heater. A tank or tankless unit. Common calls are no hot water, leaks and replacement.
  3. Supply lines. Copper, PEX or galvanized pipes that feed each fixture. Pinhole leaks and burst pipes start here; see pipe repair and repiping.
  4. Fixtures. Toilets, sinks, tubs, showers and appliances.
  5. Drain stack. The main vertical drain. A clog low in the stack backs up more than one drain and needs drain cleaning.
  6. Roof vent. Lets air into the drains so water flows and sewer gas stays out.
  7. Cleanout. A capped opening for reaching the main drain with a snake, camera or water jetter.
  8. Sewer line. The buried pipe to the city sewer or septic tank. Roots, sags and grease cause most sewer line backups.

Warning signs you need a plumber

Small signs often come before a big repair. The EPA's WaterSense program says leaks in the average home waste more than 9,300 gallons of water a year. A faucet dripping once per second can waste more than 3,000 gallons.

  • Your water bill jumps but your habits have not changed.
  • More than one drain is slow, or a toilet gurgles when the washer drains.
  • You smell sewer gas, or you see sewage in a floor drain or tub.
  • Water stains, bubbling paint or soft drywall on a ceiling or wall.
  • Hot water runs out fast, looks rusty or the tank makes popping sounds.
  • A warm spot on the floor or running water sounds with everything off, both signs of a slab leak.

Hidden leaks call for leak detection. The EPA advises drying wet areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.

Plumbing emergencies: what to do first

  • Burst or leaking pipe: close the main shutoff valve, then open a low faucet to drain the lines.
  • Overflowing toilet: turn the oval valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the water.
  • Frozen pipe: keep the faucet open and warm the pipe with a hair dryer or heating pad. The American Red Cross warns never to use a torch or other open flame.
  • Leaking water heater: close the cold-water valve on top of the heater. Turn the gas control to off or switch off the breaker.
  • Gas smell: leave the building on foot right away. Do not flip switches or start a car. PHMSA, the federal pipeline safety agency, says to call 911 once you are a safe distance away. Then call your gas utility.

What affects the cost of a plumbing repair

We do not publish prices. These factors move the number on your estimate.

  • The problem itself: a sink clog takes far less time than a collapsed sewer line.
  • Access: pipes behind tile, under a slab or in a tight crawl space take longer to reach.
  • Parts and equipment: a tankless heater costs more than a tank, and camera work adds equipment time.
  • Permits and code updates: many water heater and gas jobs need a permit.
  • Timing: nights, weekends and holidays can cost more than a weekday visit.
