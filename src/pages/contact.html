Call for plumbing help

Fastest option: call {{phone}}. Have the property address and ZIP code ready. Tell us what is happening, where in the home it is, and whether water is still running.

Before you call: 5 steps that limit damage

  1. Smell gas? Get out first. PHMSA, the federal pipeline safety agency, says to leave the area on foot right away and call 911 from a safe distance. Do not flip light switches, unplug anything or start a car on the way out. Then call your gas company.
  2. Stop the water. Close the shutoff valve under the sink or behind the toilet. If the leak is in a wall, ceiling or main line, close the main shutoff valve where the water enters the house.
  3. Keep clear of water near electricity. If water is near outlets, cords or the electrical panel, stay out of it. Switch off power to that area only if you can reach the breaker from a dry spot.
  4. Frozen pipe? Use gentle heat. The American Red Cross says to keep the faucet open and warm the pipe with a hair dryer, heating pad or warm towels, never a torch or other open flame.
  5. Take photos. Photograph the leak, the wet areas and any damaged items before cleanup. Your insurance company may ask for them.

Then start drying. The EPA says water-damaged areas and items should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth.
