Plumbing Glossary

Plumbers, inspectors and insurers use a lot of jargon. Here is what the most common plumbing terms mean, in plain words.

  • A–Z
  • Plain-English definitions
  • Linked to full guides

Quick answer: This plumbing glossary defines the terms you are most likely to hear from a plumber, a home inspector or an insurance adjuster, from P-traps and flappers to PRVs, PEX and cured-in-place pipe. Each definition links to the guide or service page that covers it in depth. Use the letters below to jump to a term.

ABCDEFGHLMOPRSTW

A

Aerator
The small screen screwed onto the end of a faucet spout. It mixes air into the stream and catches grit, and a clogged one is a common cause of weak flow at one faucet. Low water pressure in the house
Anode rod
A metal rod inside a tank water heater that corrodes on purpose so the steel tank doesn't. When it is used up, the tank starts to rust. How long a water heater lasts

B

Backflow
Water flowing the wrong way, from a sprinkler system, boiler or business process back into the drinking water supply. Backflow testing
Backflow preventer
A valve assembly that lets water flow only one way so contaminated water can't siphon or be pushed back into the public supply. Many are tested every year. Backflow testing
Backwater valve
A one-way valve on a building's sewer line that lets waste flow out but closes if sewage tries to flow back in during heavy rain. Sewer line repair
Ball valve
A valve with a lever handle and a ball inside that closes with a quarter turn. It is the most reliable type for a main water shutoff. How to turn off water to your house
Ballcock
An older style of toilet fill valve that uses a float ball on an arm to shut off the water when the tank is full. How to fix a running toilet
Belly (sewer line)
A sag in a buried sewer pipe where the soil has settled. Waste and water collect in the low spot and cause repeat clogs. Sewer line repair

C

Camera inspection
A waterproof video camera pushed through a drain or sewer line to show roots, cracks, sags and clogs before any digging. Sewer line repair
Cast-iron drain pipe
Heavy metal drain and sewer pipe common in homes built before the 1970s. It slowly corrodes inside, which narrows the pipe and leads to cracks and backups. Sewer line repair
Cleanout
A capped opening in a drain or sewer line that gives a plumber access for a snake, camera or water jetter. How to unclog a drain
Closet auger
A short drain snake made for toilets, with a rubber-sleeved bend that protects the bowl. How to unclog a drain
Copper pipe
Rigid metal water pipe used in most American homes since the 1960s. It lasts a long time but can develop pinhole leaks in some water conditions. Polybutylene, galvanized, PEX and copper pipes
CPVC
Chlorinated polyvinyl chloride: a rigid cream-colored plastic pipe used for hot and cold water since the 1980s. It can turn brittle with age and heat. Polybutylene, galvanized, PEX and copper pipes
Cross-connection
Any point where drinking water piping connects to a system that could contaminate it, such as irrigation, a boiler or a pool. Backflow testing
CSST
Corrugated stainless steel tubing: flexible gas pipe with a yellow or black jacket. Manufacturers and codes require it to be bonded to the electrical system. Gas line repair
Curb stop
A buried shutoff valve near the property line, usually under a small cover, that controls water to the whole property. It often needs a long key. How to turn off water to your house
Cured-in-place pipe (CIPP)
A trenchless repair where a resin-soaked liner is pulled into an old pipe and hardened, forming a new pipe inside the old one. Sewer line repair

D

Dip tube
A plastic tube inside a tank water heater that carries cold water to the bottom of the tank. A broken one mixes cold water into the hot supply. Water heater repair
Drain snake (auger)
A coiled steel cable turned by hand or machine to break up or pull out a clog in a drain pipe. How to unclog a drain
Drain-waste-vent system (DWV)
The network of pipes that carries used water and waste out of the house by gravity and lets air in through roof vents so drains flow and traps hold. Sewer smell in the house

E

Expansion tank
A small tank on the water heater's cold line that absorbs the extra volume water takes up when it heats. Many codes require one in homes with a pressure regulator or check valve. Water heater installation

F

Fill valve
The valve in a toilet tank that refills it after a flush and shuts off when a float reaches the set water level. How to fix a running toilet
First-hour rating
How many gallons of hot water a tank water heater can deliver in one busy hour. It is the key number for sizing a tank. Water heater installation
Flapper
The rubber seal at the bottom of a toilet tank that lifts to flush and drops to hold water. A worn flapper is the most common cause of a running toilet. How to fix a running toilet
Flush valve
The large opening at the bottom of a toilet tank, sealed by the flapper, that releases water into the bowl. How to fix a running toilet

G

Galvanized pipe
Steel water pipe coated with zinc, common before the 1960s. It rusts inside over time, which lowers water pressure and can turn water brown. Polybutylene, galvanized, PEX and copper pipes
Gate valve
An older shutoff valve with a round handle that lowers a gate inside the pipe. Old ones can seize or break if forced. How to turn off water to your house
Grease trap (interceptor)
A tank on a commercial kitchen's drain that catches fats, oils and grease before they reach the sewer. Commercial plumbing

H

Hard water
Water with high levels of calcium and magnesium. The USGS classifies water above 120 mg/L as hard; it leaves scale in pipes, fixtures and water heaters. Water softener installation
Heat pump water heater
A tank water heater that moves heat from the surrounding air into the water, using far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Tankless vs. tank water heater
Hose bib
An outdoor faucet for a garden hose, also called a sillcock or spigot. Frost-free models shut off inside the heated part of the house. How to keep pipes from freezing
Hydro jetting
Cleaning a drain or sewer line with water at very high pressure to scour away grease, scale and roots. Hydro jetting

L

Lead service line
A water service pipe made of lead. The EPA says these lines are typically the most significant source of lead in drinking water where they exist. Water line repair

M

Main shutoff valve
The valve where the water service enters the house that turns off water to the whole home. How to turn off water to your house

O

Overflow tube
The open pipe in the middle of a toilet's flush valve that keeps the tank from overflowing by draining extra water into the bowl. How to fix a running toilet

P

P-trap
The U-shaped pipe under a sink or tub that holds water to block sewer gas. A dry P-trap is a common cause of sewer smells. Sewer smell in the house
PEX
Cross-linked polyethylene: flexible plastic tubing for hot and cold water. It runs long distances with few fittings and handles freezing better than rigid pipe. Polybutylene, galvanized, PEX and copper pipes
Pilot light
A small flame on some gas water heaters and appliances that lights the main burner. A pilot that won't stay lit often points to a bad thermocouple. Water heater repair
Pipe bursting
A trenchless method that pulls new pipe through an old buried line, breaking the old pipe outward as it goes. Sewer line repair
Polybutylene pipe
Gray, blue or black flexible plastic water pipe used from the late 1970s to the mid-1990s, often stamped PB2110. It has a history of sudden failures. Polybutylene pipes
Pressure reducing valve (PRV)
A valve on the main water line that lowers high street pressure to a safe level for the house. Plumbing codes generally require one above 80 psi. Low water pressure in the house
Psi
Pounds per square inch, the unit for water pressure. Many homes run between about 40 and 80 psi. Low water pressure in the house

R

Reduced pressure zone (RPZ) assembly
A backflow preventer for high-hazard connections, with two check valves and a relief valve between them. It must be tested regularly. Backflow testing

S

Sediment
Minerals and debris that settle at the bottom of a water heater tank. Heavy sediment causes popping noises and shortens tank life. Water heater leaking
Septic system
An on-site wastewater system with a buried tank and drain field, used where there is no city sewer. Sewer smell in the house
Sewer gas
The mix of gases from decaying waste in drains and sewers. Its rotten-egg smell comes mainly from hydrogen sulfide. Sewer smell in the house
Sewer lateral
The privately owned sewer pipe that runs from a house to the public sewer main. Sewer line repair
Slab leak
A leak in a water line under or inside a concrete floor slab. Warm floor spots and a moving water meter are common signs. Slab leak repair
Sump pump
A pump in a pit in the basement or crawl space floor that pushes groundwater out and away from the house. Sump pump repair and installation

T

T&P relief valve
The temperature and pressure relief valve near the top of a tank water heater. It opens to release water if the tank gets too hot or the pressure climbs too high. Water heater leaking
Tankless water heater
A water heater that heats water only as it flows through, instead of storing it in a tank. Also called on-demand or instantaneous. Tankless vs. tank water heater
Thermocouple
A safety sensor on many gas water heaters that keeps the gas valve open only while the pilot flame is lit. Water heater repair
Trenchless repair
Fixing or replacing buried pipe through small access pits instead of a long trench, using lining or pipe bursting. Sewer line repair

W

Water backup coverage
An insurance add-on that covers damage from sewer or drain backups and sump pump overflow, which standard home policies usually exclude. Does homeowners insurance cover plumbing?
Water hammer
A banging sound in pipes when a valve closes suddenly and the moving water stops hard. High water pressure makes it worse. Low water pressure in the house
WaterSense
An EPA program that labels toilets, faucets and showerheads that use less water while meeting performance tests. Labeled toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush or less. Toilet repair
Wax ring
The seal between the bottom of a toilet and the drain flange in the floor. A failed wax ring leaks water and sewer gas at the toilet base. Toilet repair
