How Much Does a Plumber Cost? How Plumbers Price Jobs

By PipeMend Editorial · Published November 20, 2026 · Updated November 24, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: How much does a plumber cost? It depends on the job, how hard the pipe is to reach, parts, permits and the time of day. Most plumbers charge either an hourly rate plus a service call fee, or a flat price per job. PipeMend does not publish prices because two clogs or two water heaters are rarely the same job; a plumber diagnoses first and gives you a written estimate before work starts.

Not knowing what a repair will cost is one of the most stressful parts of a plumbing problem. Instead of guessing at numbers that change by city and by job, this guide explains how plumbers set prices and what moves your bill up or down, so you can read a quote with confidence. What plumbers earn

Labor is a big part of any bill. For context, the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics says plumbers, pipefitters and steamfitters had a median pay of $30.67 an hour, or $63,800 a year, in May 2025. That is the plumber's wage, not what a company charges you. A company's price also covers the truck, tools, insurance, licensing, training, office staff, warranty work and drive time. The BLS also notes that most states and some localities require plumbers to be licensed, often after 2 to 5 years of experience and an exam.

10 factors that change what a plumber costs

  1. The problem. A sink clog takes far less time than a collapsed sewer line.
  2. Access. Pipes behind tile, under a slab or in a tight crawl space take longer to reach and restore.
  3. Diagnosis. Hidden leaks and main line clogs may need a camera, leak detection gear or a jetter.
  4. Parts and equipment. A tankless water heater costs more than a tank; brass valves cost more than plastic.
  5. Permits and inspections. Many water heater, gas line, sewer and repipe jobs need a permit.
  6. Code updates. Older homes may need expansion tanks, new shutoffs or venting brought up to current code.
  7. Timing. Nights, weekends and holidays usually cost more.
  8. Location. Labor and permit costs vary widely between cities and rural areas.
  9. Experience and license level. Master plumbers and specialists, such as gas fitters, may charge more.
  10. Restoration. Drywall, flooring, concrete or landscaping repair after the plumbing is fixed.

Which plumbing jobs cost more, and which cost less?

Without quoting prices that vary by city, here is how common jobs compare in effort and cost, from simpler to more involved:

  • Usually simpler: replacing a toilet flapper or fill valve, clearing a sink clog, fixing a dripping faucet, replacing a shutoff valve. See how to fix a running toilet and how to unclog a drain first.
  • Moderate: water heater repair, toilet replacement, garbage disposal replacement, main drain clearing, a pressure reducing valve.
  • More involved: water heater replacement, especially switching to tankless; gas line work; sump pump installation; leak detection.
  • Major: slab leak repair, sewer line replacement, water service line replacement and whole-house repiping.

How to get a fair plumbing quote

  • Describe the problem clearly when you call: what you see, hear or smell, and when it started.
  • Ask how pricing works: service fee, hourly or flat rate, and after-hours charges.
  • Get it in writing before work starts, with parts, labor, permits and cleanup listed.
  • Ask about repair vs. replace options and the cost of each.
  • Check the license with your state or local licensing authority. Our state pages name each one.
  • Ask about the warranty on parts and labor.
  • For big jobs, compare two or three estimates for the same scope.

Ways to keep plumbing costs down

  • Fix small leaks early. The EPA says household leaks waste about 9,300 gallons a year in the average home.
  • Know your shutoff valve so a leak doesn't become water damage; see how to turn off water to your house.
  • Maintain your water heater. The DOE suggests draining a quart every three months to clear sediment.
  • Bundle small jobs into one visit.
  • Prevent freezes and clogs with the steps in our frozen pipe guide.
  • Check your insurance for covered water damage; see does homeowners insurance cover plumbing.

Red flags in a plumbing quote

  • No written estimate, or a price that changes after work starts without explanation.
  • Pressure to decide on a big job immediately when water is under control.
  • No license number, or a license that doesn't show up in the state lookup.
  • A plan to skip a permit that your city requires.
  • Cash only, with no receipt.

What should a plumbing estimate include?

A clear estimate protects you and the plumber. For anything beyond a small repair, look for:

  • A description of the problem and the cause the plumber found.
  • The exact work to be done, with the parts and materials listed.
  • Labor, whether flat rate or estimated hours.
  • Permit and inspection fees, if any.
  • What happens to walls, floors or landscaping that must be opened.
  • How changes in scope will be handled and priced.
  • The warranty on parts and labor.

Emergency vs. scheduled visits

Some problems can't wait: a burst pipe, sewage backing up, no water at all, a leaking water heater or a gas smell. Others can safely wait for a weekday appointment, such as a dripping faucet, a slow sink or a running toilet you can shut off at the valve. If you can stop the water and keep using the house, scheduling the visit usually costs less. When in doubt, call and describe what you see; a plumber can tell you whether it needs a visit tonight.

Why we don't post plumbing prices

Price lists online often show national averages that may not match your city, your home or your problem. A "water heater replacement" can be a simple swap or a job that needs new venting, a gas line and code updates. Rather than quote a number that could mislead you, a plumber looks at the job and puts the price in writing before any work starts.

Local note: Costs vary with local labor, permits and housing. Older homes in New York and Massachusetts often need code updates, slab homes in Texas and Arizona add access work for leaks, and long drives in rural Montana affect trip charges.

When should you call a plumber?

  • Water is leaking and you can't stop it.
  • A DIY fix didn't work or made things worse.
  • The job involves gas, a water heater, a sewer line or a permit.
  • You need a written estimate to compare or for insurance.

Plumber cost questions

Why do plumbers charge a service call fee?

A service call or trip fee covers travel, the truck and the time to diagnose the problem. Some plumbers apply it toward the repair if you go ahead. Ask how it works when you call.

Is it cheaper to call a plumber during business hours?

Usually. Nights, weekends and holidays often cost more. If water is under control and it can wait, a weekday visit may save money; if water is spreading, call now.

Do plumbers charge by the hour or by the job?

Both are common. Many residential plumbers use flat-rate pricing for standard jobs and hourly rates for diagnosis or unusual work. Ask which applies before work starts.

Should I buy my own parts to save money?

Some plumbers will install customer parts, but many won't warranty them, and the wrong part can cost a second visit. Ask first.

How much does a plumber charge to unclog a drain or toilet?

It depends on where the clog is. A sink or toilet clog cleared with a plunger or hand auger is one of the lower-priced jobs. A main line clog that needs a machine, a camera or hydro jetting costs more, and after-hours visits add to it. Ask for the price before work starts.
