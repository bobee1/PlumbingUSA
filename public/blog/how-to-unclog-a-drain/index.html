How to Unclog a Drain: 7 Methods, From Easiest to Toughest

By PipeMend Editorial · Published November 20, 2026 · Updated November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: To unclog a drain, start with the simplest tool: pull out hair and gunk from the stopper, then use a plunger. If that fails, clean the P-trap under the sink or feed a hand drain snake into the pipe. Skip harsh chemical cleaners; they can damage pipes and rarely clear solid clogs. If several drains are slow at once, the main sewer line is likely blocked and needs a plumber.

A slow sink or a shower that leaves you standing in water is one of the most common plumbing problems, and most of the time you can fix it in under an hour. This guide goes from the easiest method to the toughest, so you only use as much effort as the clog needs. If the drain won't budge, PipeMend sends licensed plumbers for drain cleaning with professional cables, cameras and water jetters.

What causes most drain clogs?

Different drains clog for different reasons, which tells you where to start.

DrainUsual causeBest first try
Bathroom sinkHair, toothpaste, soap scumClean the pop-up stopper
Shower or tubHair and soapRemove the strainer, use a plastic drain tool
Kitchen sinkGrease, food scraps, coffee groundsPlunge, then clean the P-trap
ToiletToo much paper, wipes, objectsFlange plunger, then a closet auger
Floor or basement drainSediment, or a main line clogCall a plumber if water backs up
Several drains at onceMain sewer line blockageStop using water and call a plumber

The EPA lists grease and so-called "flushable" wipes among the main causes of sewer blockages, and its grease fact sheet names grease as the most common cause of reported blockages.

How to unclog a drain: 7 methods

  1. Clear the stopper or strainer. Pull out a bathroom sink's pop-up stopper or unscrew the shower strainer. Hair wrapped around it is often the whole clog.
  2. Use a plastic drain tool. A cheap barbed plastic strip pulls hair out of sink and tub drains. Push it in, twist and pull slowly.
  3. Plunge it. Use a cup plunger for sinks and tubs and a flange plunger for toilets. Cover the overflow hole with a wet rag, add enough water to cover the cup, and give 10 to 15 firm pumps.
  4. Clean the P-trap. The curved pipe under a sink holds water to block sewer gas, and it catches debris. Put a bucket under it, loosen the slip nuts by hand, empty and rinse it, then reassemble.
  5. Use a hand drain snake. Feed the cable into the drain or the pipe behind the P-trap until you hit resistance, lock it, then crank and push to break up or grab the clog.
  6. Try hot water and dish soap for a kitchen grease clog in a metal pipe. Don't use boiling water on plastic pipe.
  7. Finish with baking soda and vinegar, then a hot tap-water rinse, to freshen the drain once the clog is out.

How to snake a drain

A drain snake (also called an auger) is a coiled steel cable you turn by hand or with a drill. To snake a sink drain, it is usually easiest to remove the P-trap and go straight into the pipe in the wall. Push gently, turn the handle clockwise, and don't force it around tight bends. When the cable stops, work it back and forth, then pull it out slowly and clean what it brings up. For toilets, use a closet auger with a rubber guard so the cable doesn't scratch the bowl.

Is Drano bad for pipes?

Chemical drain cleaners can do more harm than good. Caustic and acid cleaners make heat as they work, which can soften plastic pipe, eat at old metal pipe and damage the glaze on toilets. They often don't clear solid clogs like hair or wipes, and then the chemicals sit in the drain where they can burn skin and eyes. If you have used one, tell your plumber before they open the drain. Enzyme cleaners are gentler but work slowly and are better for upkeep than for clearing a blockage.

When a clog means a bigger problem

One slow drain is usually a local clog. These signs point to a main line or sewer problem:

  • Several drains are slow or back up at the same time.
  • Water comes up in the tub when you flush the toilet.
  • The basement floor drain overflows.
  • Toilets gurgle when the washer drains.
  • A sewage smell comes from drains; see our guide to a sewer smell in the house.

Main line clogs are usually tree roots, grease or a broken pipe. A plumber can clear them with a large cable machine or hydro jetting, and a camera shows whether you need sewer line repair. The EPA describes jetting as a common way to clear grease and debris from sewers.

How to unclog a toilet

Use a flange plunger, which has a rubber sleeve that seals into the toilet drain. If the bowl is close to overflowing, close the shutoff valve behind the toilet first. If plunging doesn't work, use a closet auger. Never pour chemical cleaners into a toilet. If the toilet also runs or leaks at the base, see our guide on how to fix a running toilet.

How to prevent clogged drains

  • Put hair catchers in showers and tubs.
  • Collect cooking grease in a can, not the sink.
  • Throw wipes in the trash, even "flushable" ones.
  • Keep coffee grounds, eggshells and fibrous peels out of the disposal.
  • Run hot tap water for a minute after kitchen use.
  • Fill rarely used drains with water now and then so the trap doesn't dry out.

Local note: Tree roots in old clay sewer laterals are a common cause of main line clogs in Georgia and Oregon. Older homes in Florida often have cast-iron drains that corrode inside and catch debris, and Illinois basements are prone to floor drain backups in heavy rain.

When should you call a plumber?

  • A plunger and a hand snake didn't clear the drain.
  • More than one drain is slow or backing up.
  • Sewage is coming up any drain.
  • You've already poured a chemical cleaner into standing water.
  • The same drain clogs again within weeks.

Drain clog questions

Can boiling water unclog a drain?

Hot water can loosen a soft grease or soap clog in a metal drain. Don't pour boiling water into PVC pipe or a porcelain toilet, and don't rely on it for hair clogs, which need to be pulled out.

Does baking soda and vinegar really work?

It can freshen a slow drain and loosen light buildup, but it rarely clears a solid clog. Use it after you've removed the clog with a plunger or snake, followed by a flush of hot tap water.

How do I unclog a drain with standing water?

Bail out as much water as you can, then plunge. Don't add chemical cleaners to standing water; if they don't work, you or a plumber will be working in a sink full of caustic liquid.

How often should drains be professionally cleaned?

Most homes only need professional drain cleaning when a clog won't clear. Kitchens with heavy grease, older cast-iron lines and homes with tree roots near the sewer may benefit from regular maintenance.

Why does my drain gurgle?

Gurgling usually means air is being pulled through a trap because a drain or vent is partly blocked. If several fixtures gurgle at once, the main line or vent may be clogged.
