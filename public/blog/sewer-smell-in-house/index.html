Sewer Smell in the House: Where It Comes From and How to Stop It

By PipeMend Editorial · Published November 20, 2026 · Updated November 24, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: A sewer smell in the house usually comes from a dried-out drain trap, a leaking toilet wax ring, a blocked or cracked vent pipe, a loose cleanout plug, or a broken sewer line. Run water in every unused drain first; that fixes many cases in minutes. If the smell stays, follows rain or comes with slow drains, the sewer line or vent likely needs a plumber. If you aren't sure it's sewer gas and not a gas leak, leave and call 911.

A rotten-egg smell in the house is unpleasant and worrying. The good news is that most sewer odors trace back to a handful of simple causes, and you can check many of them yourself. If the source is a cracked drain, a vent or the sewer line itself, PipeMend plumbers handle camera inspection and sewer line repair, including trenchless options.

Safety first: sewer gas or a gas leak?

Natural gas utilities add an odorant that also smells like rotten eggs. If the smell is strong, is near a gas appliance or meter, or you hear hissing, treat it as a gas leak. PHMSA, the federal pipeline safety agency, advises leaving the area, avoiding anything that could make a spark, and calling 911 and the gas company from a safe place. Come back to this guide only once gas has been ruled out.

What does sewer gas smell like?

Sewer gas is the mix of gases from decaying waste in drains and sewers. Its smell comes mainly from hydrogen sulfide. ATSDR, part of the CDC, says hydrogen sulfide is a colorless gas that can catch fire and smells like rotten eggs, and people notice it at very low levels. It also warns that at high concentrations, around 100 ppm, people can lose the ability to smell it within 2 to 15 minutes, so the smell fading is not proof the gas is gone.

How to find the source, step by step

  1. Follow your nose. Note which room is worst and whether it changes with use, rain or wind.
  2. Refill every trap. Run water in each sink, tub, shower and floor drain for a minute, including rarely used ones.
  3. Check the toilets. Look for water at the base, a toilet that rocks, or stains on the ceiling below.
  4. Clean smelly drains. Pull hair and gunk from stoppers and clean the P-trap; see how to unclog a drain.
  5. Inspect cleanouts. Make sure caps on cleanout pipes are tight and present.
  6. Listen for gurgling when fixtures drain, a sign of a vent or main line problem.
  7. Call a plumber for a smoke test or camera inspection if the source is still unknown.

Why does my bathroom smell like sewage?

In bathrooms, the top suspects are a failed toilet wax ring, a dry shower or floor drain trap, and a sink overflow channel full of buildup. If the smell is strongest at the toilet base, the seal needs replacing. If the toilet also runs or rocks, see how to fix a running toilet; a plumber can reset it and check the flange.

Why does my toilet smell like sewer?

A sewer smell at the toilet usually means a failed wax ring, a loose toilet that rocks, or a crack in the bowl or tank. Check the floor around the base for damp spots or stains. A toilet that is rarely used can also lose the water in its trap, so flush it once a week. If the toilet rocks, it needs a new wax ring and has to be reset. See our toilet repair page.

Why does my bathroom sink smell like sewer?

Most often it is slime in the overflow channel or on the pop-up stopper, not sewer gas. Pull the stopper and clean it. Then pour hot water with a little dish soap through the overflow hole. If the smell stays, the trap may be drying out or the vent may be blocked. A sink that gurgles and smells points to a vent problem.

Why does my washer or laundry room smell like sewer?

The washer hose drains into a standpipe with its own trap. If the hose is pushed too far down, it can siphon that trap dry and let sewer gas in. The hose should go only a few inches into the pipe. Laundry floor drains dry out too, so pour water into them each month. A musty smell is different; that usually comes from a moldy door seal or soap drawer.

Sewer smell in the basement

Basements combine floor drains that dry out, cleanouts, laundry sinks and the main sewer line. A dry floor drain trap is the most common cause. If the smell grows after heavy rain or the floor drain backs up, the main line may be partly blocked or broken. The EPA lists grease, wipes, tree roots and broken or leaky sewer pipes among the causes of sewer backups.

Signs of sewer line problems

  • Sewer smell in the yard or crawl space.
  • Several slow drains or backups at once.
  • Gurgling toilets when other fixtures drain.
  • Soggy, sunken or extra-green patches over the sewer line.
  • Repeat clogs soon after drain cleaning.

A video camera inspection shows roots, cracks, sags and offset joints. Repairs range from spot fixes to lining or replacement.

Is a sewer smell a health risk?

At the low levels typical of a dry trap, sewer gas is mostly a nuisance, but it isn't something to live with. ATSDR says hydrogen sulfide at about 10 ppm or less may cause eye irritation, headache and fatigue, and some people with asthma may have trouble breathing. Air out the house, fix the source, and leave if the smell is strong or anyone feels unwell. Sewage that leaks into a crawl space or basement also brings moisture; the EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.

How to prevent sewer smells

  • Run water in unused drains every couple of weeks.
  • Keep grease and wipes out of drains.
  • Keep roof vents clear of nests, leaves and snow.
  • Replace toilet seals when you replace a toilet or floor.
  • Pump septic tanks on the schedule your local health department recommends.
  • Ask about a backwater valve if your area's sewers back up in storms.

Local note: Older homes in Washington, DC and Illinois often have basement floor drains tied to sewers that back up in heavy rain. In Louisiana, settling soil cracks sewer lines, and rural homes in Maine and Vermont often trace odors to septic systems.

When should you call a plumber?

  • The smell stays after you refill every trap.
  • More than one drain is slow or gurgles.
  • Sewage backs up into a tub, shower or floor drain.
  • You smell sewage in the yard or crawl space.
  • The toilet base leaks or the toilet rocks.

Sewer smell questions

Is sewer gas dangerous?

At the low levels usually found in homes, the main effects are irritation, headache and nausea. ATSDR says hydrogen sulfide, a key part of sewer gas, can cause eye irritation, headache and fatigue at low levels. Fix the source and air out the house; leave if the smell is strong and call for help.

Why does my bathroom smell like sewage only sometimes?

Intermittent smells often come from a trap that is siphoned when another fixture drains, a partly blocked vent, or a toilet wax seal that leaks only when the toilet is flushed.

Why does my house smell like sewage after it rains?

Heavy rain can overload sewers and push gas or water back toward your house, and wind can push vent gases down. A backwater valve, a clear vent and a sound sewer line help.

Can a water heater cause a sewage smell?

A rotten-egg smell only in the hot water usually comes from the water heater's anode rod reacting with bacteria. It is different from sewer gas in the air. A plumber can swap the anode rod and flush the tank.

How do I tell sewer gas from a natural gas leak?

Both can smell like rotten eggs. If you're not sure, treat it as a gas leak: leave the building, then call 911 and your gas utility from a safe place.
