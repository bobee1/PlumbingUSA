How to Turn Off the Water to Your House in an Emergency

By PipeMend Editorial · Published November 20, 2026 · Updated November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: To turn off water to your house, find the main water shut off valve where the water line enters the home, often in the basement, a utility room, a garage or near the water heater, and close it. Turn a round gate valve clockwise until it stops, or turn a lever ball valve a quarter turn so it sits across the pipe. If you can't find it or it won't turn, close the valve at the water meter.

When a pipe bursts or a water heater starts pouring water, the fastest way to limit damage is to stop the water at the source. Every adult in the home should know where that valve is before an emergency. If the valve is stuck, leaking or missing, PipeMend sends licensed plumbers for emergency plumbing and can install a new shutoff.

Where is the main water shut off valve?

The main shutoff sits where the water service line enters the house. Where that is depends on your climate and foundation.

Home typeWhere to look first
House with a basementOn the front wall facing the street, near the water meter or where a pipe comes through the floor or wall
Slab home (no basement)Near the water heater, in a utility closet or garage, or outside on a wall where the pipe enters
Crawl-space homeUnder the house where the line enters, or in a utility room above it
Warm climatesOutside, on a wall or in a box near the foundation
Condo or apartmentUnder a sink, behind an access panel, near the water heater, or controlled by building staff

Still stuck? Follow the cold water line backward from the water heater or the kitchen sink. Your home inspection report and the property's plumbing permit may also show the location.

How to turn off water to your house, step by step

  1. Find the main valve using the table above.
  2. Identify the type. A round wheel handle is a gate (or globe) valve. A straight lever is a ball valve.
  3. Close it. Turn a gate valve clockwise until it stops; don't force it. Turn a ball valve a quarter turn so the lever is perpendicular to the pipe.
  4. Test it. Open a faucet on the lowest floor. The flow should slow and stop within a minute.
  5. Drain the lines if you are dealing with a burst pipe: open the lowest faucet and a faucet upstairs.
  6. Protect the water heater. For a long shutoff, turn its gas to pilot or switch off its breaker.

Types of water shut-off valves

Ball valves have a lever handle and close with a quarter turn. They are quick and reliable, and most new homes use them.

Gate valves have a round handle and take several turns. Old ones can seize or break if forced, and they may not seal completely after years of sitting open.

Globe valves also have a round handle. The City of Bloomington, Minnesota, notes that globe valves at the meter can restrict flow and should be fully open, and that homeowners are responsible for replacing them.

Curb stops are buried valves near the property line, under a small metal or plastic cover. They usually need a long key, and many utilities ask that only their staff turn them.

How to turn off water at the meter

If the house valve fails, the meter is the backup. Meters sit in a basement, a box in the yard or near the street. Lift the lid carefully; meter boxes can hold water, insects or snakes in some areas. Many meters have a valve on the street side with a tab that lines up with a hole. A quarter turn with a meter key or adjustable wrench closes it. If you aren't sure you are allowed to operate it, call your water utility's emergency line.

What if the shut-off valve won't turn?

  • Don't force it. An old gate valve can snap its stem, leaving you with full street pressure and no way to stop it.
  • Try gentle steady pressure with a wrench close to the stem, not on the end of the handle.
  • Go to the meter or curb stop if the house valve won't budge.
  • Call your utility if water is spreading and you can't stop it.
  • Have a plumber replace it once the emergency is over. A quarter-turn ball valve is the usual upgrade.

Fixture shut-off valves

You don't always need to shut off the whole house. Toilets, sinks, dishwashers, ice makers and washing machines usually have their own small valves, often called angle stops. Close the toilet's valve behind the bowl if it is overflowing; see how to fix a running toilet for the repair. The water heater has its own cold inlet valve on top; see what to do when a water heater leaks.

Use the main valve to check for leaks

Your shutoff and meter can also find hidden leaks. The EPA's WaterSense program suggests reading the meter, using no water for two hours, then reading it again; if it moved, there is probably a leak. If the meter still moves with the main house valve closed, the leak is between the meter and the house, in the service line.

Shut-off valve maintenance

  • Find and label your main valve today.
  • Turn it closed and open once or twice a year so it doesn't seize.
  • Keep the area clear of boxes and storage.
  • Keep a meter key near the meter if your utility allows homeowners to use it.
  • Replace an old gate valve with a ball valve during other plumbing work.
  • Shut off the water before long trips, as many insurers suggest, but keep the heat on in winter. See how to keep pipes from freezing.

Local note: In basement homes common in Illinois and New York, the main valve is usually on the front basement wall. In Arizona and Florida slab homes, look outside near the foundation or in the garage. In Washington, DC rowhouses, check the front of the basement or a vault under the front steps.

When should you call a plumber?

  • You can't find the main shutoff.
  • The valve is stuck, broken or leaking at the stem.
  • Water keeps running after the valve is closed.
  • You want an old gate valve replaced with a ball valve.
  • A pipe has burst and needs repair.

Water shutoff questions

Which way do you turn a water shut-off valve?

Turn a round gate valve handle clockwise until it stops. Turn a lever-style ball valve a quarter turn so the handle sits across the pipe instead of in line with it.

Can I turn off the water at the meter?

Often, yes. Many meters have a valve on the street side, and some have one on the house side too. Some need a meter key, and some utilities prefer that only their staff use the street-side valve, so call your utility if you're unsure.

Should I turn off my water heater when I shut off the water?

For a short shutoff, it's usually fine to leave it. For a long shutoff or if you will drain the pipes, turn the gas control to pilot or off, or switch off the breaker on an electric heater, so it doesn't heat an empty tank.

Should I shut off the water when I go on vacation?

Many insurers and plumbers recommend it for trips of more than a few days. Leave the heat on in winter, and remember that sprinklers and some fire sprinkler systems may need water.
