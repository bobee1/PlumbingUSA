How to Fix a Running Toilet: Find the Cause and Stop It

By PipeMend Editorial · Published November 20, 2026 · Updated November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: To fix a running toilet, first find which part is letting water through. Most of the time it is a worn flapper, a chain that is too short, or a fill valve set so high that water spills into the overflow tube. Replacing a flapper or adjusting the fill valve fixes most running toilets in under 30 minutes. If the flush valve seat or tank is cracked, call a plumber.

A toilet that hisses at night or refills on its own is annoying, and it quietly runs up your water bill. The good news is that this is one of the easiest plumbing repairs to do yourself. If you would rather hand it off, or the fix below doesn't work, PipeMend sends licensed local plumbers for toilet repair, from a new flapper to a full replacement.

How a toilet tank works

A standard gravity toilet has two valves in the tank. Knowing what each one does makes the problem easy to find.

  • Flush valve: the large opening at the bottom of the tank. A rubber flapper sits on top of it. When you press the handle, a chain lifts the flapper and the tank empties into the bowl.
  • Fill valve: the tall assembly on the left. It refills the tank and shuts off when a float rises to the set water level. Older toilets use a ball on an arm, often called a ballcock.
  • Overflow tube: the open pipe in the middle of the flush valve. If the water gets too high, it spills down this tube into the bowl so the tank can't overflow.

Why does my toilet keep running?

A toilet keeps running because water is escaping from the tank faster than the fill valve can stop it. Here are the usual causes, from most to least common.

Do a dye test first

Before you buy parts, check whether the flapper is the problem. The EPA's WaterSense program suggests putting a few drops of food coloring in the tank and waiting 10 minutes without flushing. If color shows up in the bowl, water is leaking past the flapper. Flush afterward so the dye doesn't stain the bowl.

How to fix a running toilet, step by step

  1. Look inside the tank. Take off the lid and flush. Watch the flapper close and the tank refill.
  2. Check the water level. It should sit about an inch below the top of the overflow tube. Many tanks have a line marked inside.
  3. Adjust the float. On most modern fill valves, turn the adjustment screw or slide the float clip down to lower the water level. On a ballcock, gently bend the float arm down.
  4. Adjust the chain. Leave about half an inch of slack when the flapper is closed. Too tight holds the flapper open; too loose can tangle under it.
  5. Replace the flapper if needed. Turn off the shutoff valve behind the toilet, flush to empty the tank, unhook the old flapper from the overflow tube ears and hook on the new one.
  6. Clean or replace the fill valve if it still hisses. Many fill valves have a cap you can remove to flush out grit. If it still won't shut off, replace the whole fill valve.
  7. Test again. Turn the water back on, flush a few times and repeat the dye test.

How to fix a toilet that keeps running after you replaced the flapper

If a new flapper still leaks, the flush valve seat underneath is probably worn, chipped or crusted with minerals. Gently scrub the seat with a nonabrasive pad. If it is cracked, the fix is a new flush valve, which means removing the tank from the bowl. At that point many homeowners call a plumber, or consider a new toilet if the old one is decades old.

Is a running toilet worth fixing right away?

Yes. The EPA says household leaks waste about 9,300 gallons a year in the average home, and a running toilet runs day and night. The fix usually costs only the price of a part. A constant refill also wears out the fill valve faster.

Running toilet vs. replacing the toilet

Repair is the right call for most running toilets. Consider replacement when the bowl or tank is cracked, parts for an old model are hard to find, or the toilet clogs often. Federal standards limit new toilets to 1.6 gallons per flush, and the EPA says WaterSense labeled toilets use 1.28 gallons or less, about 20 percent less water.

How to prevent a running toilet

  • Do the dye test once or twice a year.
  • Skip bleach tablets in the tank; they can wear rubber parts.
  • Turn the shutoff valve now and then so it doesn't seize.
  • Replace the flapper every few years, or sooner with hard water.
  • Know how to turn off the water to your house in case a valve fails.

Fixing a running dual-flush or pressure-assist toilet

Not every toilet has a flapper. Many dual-flush toilets use a canister or tower flush valve with a large rubber seal at the bottom. If one runs, the seal is usually dirty or worn; most lift out with a twist, so you can rinse or replace them. Pressure-assist toilets have a sealed tank inside the china tank. If one runs or hisses, follow the maker's instructions or call a plumber, because the parts are specific to the brand.

Local note: Hard water wears flappers and fill valves faster, so toilets in Arizona, Nevada and Utah often need new parts sooner. In older housing such as Pennsylvania rowhouses, seized shutoff valves are a common snag.

When should you call a plumber?

  • The tank or bowl is cracked.
  • The shutoff valve won't close or leaks.
  • A new flapper and fill valve didn't stop the running.
  • Water leaks at the base of the toilet.
  • The toilet rocks or the floor around it feels soft.

Running toilet questions

Why does my toilet run every few minutes on its own?

That short refill, sometimes called phantom flushing, means water is slowly leaking from the tank into the bowl. The usual cause is a worn flapper or a dirty flush valve seat. A dye test confirms it.

How much water does a running toilet waste?

It depends on the leak, but even a silent one runs all day. The EPA says leaks in the average home waste about 9,300 gallons a year, and toilets are among the most common culprits.

Can I use any flapper in my toilet?

No. Flappers come in 2-inch and 3-inch sizes and in different shapes for different brands. Take the old one to the store or check the model number stamped inside the tank.

Is it safe to leave a running toilet until morning?

Usually, yes, as long as water stays in the tank and bowl. If it is overflowing, turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise until the water stops.

Do in-tank cleaning tablets cause running toilets?

They can. Tablets with bleach can soften and warp rubber flappers and seals over time, which leads to leaks. Many plumbers suggest cleaning the bowl instead.
