Tankless vs. Tank Water Heater: How to Choose

By PipeMend Editorial · Published November 20, 2026 · Updated November 24, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: Tankless vs tank water heater comes down to how you use hot water. A tank heater stores 30 to 80 gallons and is cheaper to buy and install. A tankless heater heats water only as it flows, never runs out within its flow limit, and lasts longer. The Department of Energy says tankless units can be 24 to 34 percent more efficient in homes that use 41 gallons or less a day. Heat pump tank heaters are the most efficient electric option.

When a water heater fails, most people replace it with the same kind. That's fine, but it's also the best moment to ask whether a different type would suit you better. This guide compares the options side by side. PipeMend plumbers install and service both, from tankless water heaters to standard tank and heat pump models.

How does a tank water heater work?

A storage tank heater keeps a full tank of water hot all the time. A gas burner under the tank, or two electric elements inside it, reheat the water as it cools or as you use it. When you open a tap, hot water leaves the top and cold water enters the bottom through a dip tube. Heat lost from the tank while no one is using water is called standby loss.

How does a tankless water heater work?

A tankless heater has no storage. When you open a tap, a flow sensor starts a gas burner or electric elements, and water is heated as it passes through a heat exchanger. The DOE's Building America program describes the core parts as a high-powered burner, a fan and a heat exchanger. It says high-efficiency units can provide hot water at 2 to 5 gallons per minute. When the tap closes, the heater shuts off.

Is tankless more efficient?

Yes, especially in smaller households. The DOE says tankless heaters can be 24 to 34 percent more efficient than tanks in homes that use 41 gallons or less of hot water a day. In homes that use a lot more, around 86 gallons a day, the gain drops to 8 to 14 percent. That is because a big family keeps a tank's water moving, so less is lost to standby. Gas tankless units can also have a small standing loss from a pilot on some models.

Which lasts longer?

Tankless units last longer on paper. The DOE says most storage heaters last 10 to 15 years and tankless units can last more than 20. A tankless heater's life depends heavily on descaling in hard water areas. Read more in our guide on how long a water heater lasts.

What size tankless water heater do I need?

A tank is sized by its first-hour rating: how much hot water it can deliver in a busy hour. A tankless unit is sized by two numbers: flow in gallons per minute at your busiest moment, and temperature rise. The DOE's Building America program says the rise depends on your area's winter cold water temperature and your hot water setting. In northern states, incoming water is much colder, so the same unit delivers fewer gallons per minute than it would in the South.

Installation differences

  • Gas line: gas tankless units burn much more gas while running than a tank, so the gas line often needs upsizing.
  • Venting: new stainless or PVC venting is often required. The DOE says cold-climate units should be sealed-combustion, direct-vent or power-vented, with vents above the expected snow line.
  • Electrical: electric tankless units need large dedicated circuits, sometimes a panel upgrade.
  • Condensate: condensing gas units need a drain for acidic condensate.
  • Permits: most areas require a permit and inspection for either type.

What about heat pump water heaters?

A heat pump water heater is a tank heater that pulls heat from the surrounding air. ENERGY STAR says a labeled model can save a household of four about $550 a year on electric bills compared with a standard electric heater. It needs space, air around it and a drain for condensate, and it cools the room it sits in, which is a plus in a warm garage. It suits electric homes that don't have gas.

Which water heater is right for you?

  • Choose a tank if upfront cost matters most, you have a big household with heavy simultaneous use, or your gas line or electrical panel would need costly upgrades.
  • Choose tankless if you're short on space, want steady hot water for long showers, have moderate use and plan to stay in the home for years.
  • Choose a heat pump tank if you have an electric home, room for the unit and want the lowest running cost.

Common tankless problems to know about

Tankless owners sometimes notice a brief burst of cold water between uses, called a cold water sandwich, and units that won't fire on very low flow. Scale buildup, an undersized gas line or too many fixtures at once can all cause lukewarm water. A plumber can usually fix these with settings, descaling or sizing changes. If your tank is already leaking, start with what to do when a water heater leaks.

Local note: Cold incoming water in Minnesota and Wisconsin reduces a tankless unit's flow, so sizing matters more there. Very hard water in Nevada and Arizona makes descaling or a softener important. In Florida, warm garages suit heat pump tanks well.

When should you call a plumber?

  • Your current heater is leaking or past 10 years old.
  • You want to switch from tank to tankless or heat pump.
  • Your tankless unit shows error codes or runs lukewarm.
  • You need a gas line, venting or electrical check before deciding.

Tankless vs. tank questions

Is a tankless water heater worth it?

It can be for homes with moderate hot water use, limited space or plans to stay many years. The Department of Energy says savings are largest in homes that use 41 gallons or less of hot water a day.

Can a tankless water heater run out of hot water?

It won't run out the way a tank does, but it has a flow limit. If more fixtures are open than it can heat at once, the water cools down. Sizing to your busiest moment avoids that.

Do tankless water heaters need maintenance?

Yes. Most manufacturers call for periodic descaling, especially with hard water, plus cleaning the inlet filter and keeping vents clear.

Can I replace my tank with a tankless unit myself?

It's not a DIY job. Gas units often need a larger gas line and new venting, electric units need heavy electrical circuits, and most areas require a permit and inspection.

What is a heat pump water heater?

It is a tank heater that moves heat from the surrounding air into the water instead of making heat directly. ENERGY STAR says labeled models can save a family of four about $550 a year compared with a standard electric tank.

How do you flush a tankless water heater?

Turn off the power or gas and close the hot and cold service valves. Hook a small pump and hoses to the service ports, and run white vinegar or descaler through the unit for about an hour. Rinse with clean water, clean the inlet screen and open the valves. Follow the maker's manual for how often.

How much does a tankless water heater cost?

More up front than a tank of the same fuel type. The total depends on the unit size, gas or electric, and whether the home needs a bigger gas line, new venting or more electrical capacity. Ask for a written quote that lists each part.
