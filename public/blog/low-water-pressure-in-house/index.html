Pipes & Water Pressure

Low Water Pressure in the House: Causes, Tests and Fixes

By PipeMend Editorial · Published November 20, 2026 · Updated November 24, 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: Low water pressure in the house usually comes from a partly closed main valve, a failing pressure reducing valve (PRV), a hidden leak, clogged aerators or mineral-choked old pipes. First check whether the problem is one fixture or every faucet. One fixture usually means a clogged aerator or cartridge. The whole house points to the main valve, the PRV, a leak, old galvanized pipe or the supply from your utility or well.

Weak showers and slow-filling sinks are frustrating, and sometimes they are the first sign of a leak or worn-out pipes. The tests below narrow the cause in a few minutes. If the trouble is in the line from the street or the pipes inside your walls, PipeMend plumbers handle water line repair and replacement, and full repiping when old pipe is the problem.

Step 1: One fixture or the whole house?

Run cold water at several faucets, upstairs and down, and at an outdoor hose bib. Then compare hot and cold at the same faucet.

What you noticeMost likely cause
One faucet or showerhead is weakClogged aerator, showerhead or cartridge
Only hot water is weakWater heater valve, sediment or a scaled tankless unit
Every fixture is weak, suddenlyMain valve partly closed, PRV failure, utility problem or a big leak
Every fixture is weak, slowly worse over yearsScale inside galvanized pipe or a failing PRV
Weak only when two things run at onceUndersized pipes or service line
Weak or sputtering on a wellPressure switch, pressure tank or well pump

Step 2: Test your water pressure

A screw-on pressure gauge from a hardware store fits an outdoor hose bib or a washing machine valve. Turn off all water in the house, attach the gauge, open the valve and read the static pressure. The City of Bloomington, Minnesota, lists 35 psi as adequate, 60 to 70 psi as optimal, and says a pressure regulating valve is needed at 80 psi. If your reading is low at the hose bib nearest the meter, the problem is upstream: the main valve, the PRV, the service line or the utility.

Why is my water pressure low? 10 causes

  1. Clogged aerators and showerheads. Minerals and grit collect in the screens. Unscrew them and soak in vinegar. Low-flow fixtures are normal: the EPA says WaterSense labeled bathroom faucets use no more than 1.5 gallons per minute, so they will feel gentler than old 2.2 gpm faucets.
  2. Main shutoff valve partly closed. Often left that way after a repair. Make sure it is fully open. Bloomington's handout also says the globe valves at the meter should be fully open; see how to find your main shut-off valve.
  3. Failing pressure reducing valve. A PRV lowers street pressure to a safe level. When one fails, pressure can drop or spike. It can often be adjusted, or else repaired or replaced.
  4. Hidden leaks. A leak in the service line, under a slab or in a wall diverts water. The EPA suggests reading your meter, using no water for two hours and reading it again; if it moved, you probably have a leak.
  5. Old galvanized steel pipe. These pipes rust and scale inside until the opening is a fraction of its original size. Bloomington notes that scale buildup in galvanized pipe restricts flow, and the fix is replacing the affected piping.
  6. Hard water scale. The USGS classifies water above 120 mg/L as hard. Scale narrows pipes, valves and fixtures over time.
  7. Water softener or filter problems. A clogged filter cartridge or softener restricts flow. Bypass it to test.
  8. Water heater issues for hot-side-only problems: a partly closed valve, sediment or a scaled tankless heat exchanger.
  9. Undersized pipes or service line, especially after adding bathrooms or irrigation.
  10. Utility or well supply. Main breaks, peak demand and high elevation lower city pressure. On a well, check the pressure switch, tank and pump.

How to fix low water pressure

  • Clean aerators and showerheads in vinegar overnight.
  • Open the main valve fully and check valves at the meter.
  • Test and adjust the PRV, or have a plumber replace it.
  • Find and fix leaks with professional leak detection if the meter moves with everything off.
  • Replace galvanized or undersized pipe. Learn your options in our guide to pipe types: polybutylene, galvanized, PEX and copper.
  • Service the water heater or softener if only part of the system is affected.
  • Ask your utility whether there is a known problem or a planned pressure change.
  • Add a booster pump only after confirming the street or well supply is the cause.

Could old pipes contain lead?

Possibly, if your home is older. The EPA warns that lead particles can attach to the surface of galvanized pipes and later enter the water. If you have galvanized pipe or an old service line, test your water and ask your utility what your service line is made of. Replacing old pipe fixes both the pressure and that risk.

High water pressure: the opposite problem

Pressure that is too high is also a problem. It can make pipes bang (water hammer), cause toilets to run and make the water heater's relief valve drip. Bloomington's handout says a pressure regulating valve is required by code above 80 psi. If your gauge reads above 80, have a PRV installed or adjusted. Thermal expansion from a water heater in a closed system may also call for an expansion tank; see our guide to a leaking water heater.

Low water pressure in the shower only

If the shower is weak but the bathroom sink is fine, the problem is almost always in the shower itself. Start with the showerhead: unscrew it, soak it in vinegar and clear the holes with a toothpick. Many showerheads also have a small flow restrictor or screen at the inlet that clogs with grit. If a new showerhead doesn't help, the cartridge or mixing valve in the wall may be scaled or failing. A pressure-balancing valve can also cut flow if one side, usually the hot, is restricted. A plumber can replace the cartridge without opening the wall in most cases.

Low pressure in the kitchen sink

Pull-down kitchen faucets have a hose and a check valve inside the spray head, and both can trap debris. Take off the spray head and run the water into a bucket. If the flow is strong without the head, clean or replace the head. If it's still weak, check the two shutoff valves under the sink; they may be only partly open or failing inside. Old compression-style shutoffs are a common culprit and are cheap to replace.

Low pressure after plumbing work or a main break

When the water is shut off and turned back on, sediment and rust flakes can break loose and lodge in aerators, toilet fill valves and appliance inlet screens. If pressure dropped right after a repair or a utility main break, clean every aerator and check the inlet screens on the washing machine and dishwasher. Also confirm that whoever did the work fully reopened the main valve and any valves at the meter.

Low pressure on a private well

Homes on wells get their pressure from a pump and a pressure tank, not the city. A pressure switch turns the pump on at a low setting and off at a high one. Low or pulsing pressure can mean a failed pressure switch, a waterlogged pressure tank, a clogged sediment filter or a pump wearing out. Bloomington's handout lists 50 psi as typical for a well. A plumber or well contractor can test the tank's air charge and the switch settings.

How to check for a hidden leak yourself

  1. Turn off every fixture and appliance, including the ice maker and irrigation.
  2. Read the water meter, or note the position of its small leak indicator.
  3. Wait two hours without using any water, as the EPA suggests.
  4. Read it again. Any movement means water is going somewhere.
  5. Close the main house valve and repeat. If the meter still moves, the leak is in the service line between the meter and the house.

Is low water pressure a sign of a bigger problem?

Sometimes. A single clogged aerator is just maintenance. But pressure that drops suddenly across the whole house, along with a higher water bill, points to a leak that may be soaking a slab, a wall or the yard. Pressure that has faded slowly over years in an older home usually means the pipes themselves are near the end of their life. Brown or rusty water, banging pipes or a water heater relief valve that drips are other signs worth a plumber's visit. Catching these early keeps a small repair from becoming water damage.

Local note: Galvanized supply pipe is common in older homes in Ohio, Pennsylvania and Massachusetts, and it is a top cause of whole-house low pressure there. Very hard water in Utah and Nevada clogs aerators and valves quickly. Rural homes in Maine often trace low pressure to well pumps and pressure tanks.

When should you call a plumber?

  • Pressure is low at every fixture and the main valve is fully open.
  • Your meter moves with all water off.
  • Your gauge reads below 35 psi or above 80 psi.
  • You have galvanized or polybutylene pipe.
  • Water is rusty, brown or tastes metallic.

Water pressure questions

What is normal water pressure for a house?

Many utilities describe about 40 to 80 psi as normal, with 60 to 70 psi a comfortable target. The City of Bloomington, Minnesota, lists 60 to 70 psi as optimal and says a pressure regulating valve is needed at 80 psi.

Can a water softener cause low water pressure?

Yes. A clogged softener or one sized too small can restrict flow. Bloomington's handout suggests opening the softener's bypass valve; if pressure improves, the softener is the cause.

Why is only my hot water pressure low?

Usually the problem is at the water heater: a partly closed valve, sediment in the tank or outlet, or a failing dip tube. A tankless unit with scale can also restrict hot water flow.

Will a water pressure booster pump fix low pressure?

It can raise pressure when the supply from the street or a well is truly low. It won't fix clogged pipes or leaks, so find the cause first.

Is high water pressure a problem too?

Yes. Pressure above 80 psi strains pipes, water heaters, toilets and appliances, and can make faucets bang and relief valves drip. A pressure reducing valve fixes it.
