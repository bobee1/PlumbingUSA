Why plumbing in Tucson is different

Most Tucson homes are built on slabs, with supply lines under the floor, and slab leaks are common. Older central neighborhoods such as Sam Hughes and Armory Park have aging galvanized and cast-iron pipes.

Hard water scales up water heaters and fixtures. Tucson is higher and cooler at night than Phoenix, so winter cold snaps freeze outdoor and exposed pipes.

Pipes and housing age in Tucson

More than half of Tucson's homes (52.5%) went up before 1980. Cast-iron drains and aging supply lines are a big share of repair calls. Homes from the late 1970s to the mid-1990s may also have gray polybutylene pipe, which has a history of failures. See pipe repair and repiping and our guide to pipe types.

Water quality and supply in Tucson

Tucson Water delivers Colorado River water through the Central Arizona Project, recharged and blended with groundwater. The water is hard, so scale builds up in water heaters, tankless units and fixtures.

Plumbing tips for Tucson homes

  • Flush your water heater yearly.
  • Wrap exposed outdoor pipes before cold nights.
  • Watch your water bill for slab leak signs.
  • Clean aerators and showerheads often.

Plumbing questions in Tucson

Do you have plumbers in all of Tucson?

Yes. PipeMend takes plumbing calls from across Tucson and Pima County, including Downtown, Sam Hughes, Catalina Foothills, Midtown and Rita Ranch, and ZIP codes such as 85701, 85705, 85710, 85712.

Do I need a permit for plumbing work in Tucson?

For bigger jobs, usually yes. Water heater replacement, gas lines, sewer or water line work and repiping generally need a Tucson permit and inspection. Simple repairs such as a faucet or toilet usually don't.

How fast can a plumber get to me in Tucson?

In Tucson, it depends on traffic, the time of day and how many calls are open. You get an honest time window when you call. If water is spreading, shut off the main valve first.

Do pipes freeze in Tucson?

Sometimes. Tucson's winter nights can drop below freezing, especially on the east side and in the foothills. Wrap exposed outdoor pipes and backflow devices, and disconnect hoses before cold nights.
