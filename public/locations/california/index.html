Why plumbing in California is different

California law requires water heaters to be braced or strapped against earthquakes, and many homes also have seismic gas shutoff valves. Older homes in Los Angeles, San Francisco and Oakland still have galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drains that rust and crack.

Southern California, the Central Valley and the Inland Empire have hard water that scales up fixtures and water heaters. Drought rules and high water prices make leaks and old fixtures costly. Plumbing contractors are licensed by the Contractors State License Board.

Pipes, drains and housing age in California

56% of the housing in California is more than 45 years old. Cast-iron drain stacks and older copper with lead solder are common in these homes. Homes built before 1986 are more likely to have lead pipes, fixtures or solder, according to the EPA. If yours is that old, test the water and ask your utility what your service line is made of. Homes built from the late 1970s to the mid-1990s may also have gray polybutylene supply pipe, which has a history of failures. See pipe repair and repiping, sewer line repair and water line repair.

Water heaters and gas lines in California

60.6% of homes here heat with natural gas, so gas water heaters and gas line work are a large share of plumbing calls. Most storage water heaters last 10 to 15 years, according to the Department of Energy. See water heater repair and tankless water heaters.

Storms, flooding and sewer backups in California

California has had 30 water-related federal disaster declarations since 2000. The most recent was in 2024 (severe winter storms, tornadoes, flooding, landslides and mudslides). Storms push sewage back up floor drains and flood low areas; a working sump pump and a backwater valve on the sewer line help.

How to protect your plumbing in California

  • Make sure your water heater has earthquake straps.
  • Know how to shut off the gas at the meter.
  • Fix leaks fast; water is expensive in most of California.
  • Have older sewer lines inspected with a camera.

Plumbing licenses and permits in California

In California, the Contractors State License Board (CSLB) licenses plumbing contractors. Its online lookup shows whether a license is active. Plumbing contractors need a CSLB license with a C-36 plumbing classification.

Permits in California come from the city or county building department. Water heaters, gas piping, sewer laterals and repipes usually need one; simple fixture repairs usually do not.

Plumbing questions in California

Do you have plumbers in all of California?

Yes. PipeMend takes plumbing calls from all of California, including Los Angeles, San Diego, San Jose, San Francisco and Fresno, plus small towns and rural areas, 24 hours a day.

Do plumbers need a license in California?

Plumbing contractors are licensed by the Contractors State License Board (CSLB), and some cities add their own rules. Check the license number before work starts.

Do I need a permit for plumbing work in California?

Often, yes. Most local building departments in California require permits for water heaters, gas lines, sewer and water service lines and repiping. Small repairs such as a faucet or toilet usually do not need one.

Do California water heaters need earthquake straps?

Yes. California requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped to resist falling or moving during an earthquake. A plumber installing or replacing a water heater adds the straps as part of the job.
