Local plumbing conditions in Washington, DC

Much of the District's housing was built before 1940. Rowhouses often still have galvanized supply pipes, cast-iron stacks and old clay sewer laterals running under the front yard to the street. DC Water has an active program to replace lead service lines, and homeowners can ask what their line is made of.

Parts of the District are served by combined sewers that carry both storm water and sewage. During cloudbursts, basements in low areas can take on sewage through floor drains. Backwater valves, sump pumps and sewer line repairs are common basement jobs.

Pipes, drains and housing age in Washington, DC

31.5% of homes in the District of Columbia were built before 1940. Many still have galvanized steel supply pipes, cast-iron drains or clay sewer laterals, which rust, crack and let roots in. Homes built before 1986 are more likely to have lead pipes, fixtures or solder, according to the EPA. If yours is that old, test the water and ask your utility what your service line is made of. Water heaters and gas lines in Washington, DC

Natural gas heats 45% of homes in the District of Columbia, and gas homes usually have gas water heaters too. That means burners, venting and gas lines to keep safe. Most storage water heaters last 10 to 15 years, according to the Department of Energy. See water heater repair and tankless water heaters.

Storms, flooding and sewer backups in Washington, DC

Since 2000, FEMA has declared 10 water-related major disasters in the District of Columbia, from floods, hurricanes and severe storms. The most recent was in 2016 (snowstorm). Heavy rain overloads sewers and fills basements, so sump pumps, backwater valves and sewer line repairs matter here.

How to protect your plumbing in Washington, DC

  • Ask DC Water what your service line is made of and whether you qualify for replacement help.
  • Consider a backwater valve if your basement has flooded during storms.
  • Do not pour grease down the drain; it clogs old cast-iron lines.
  • Find your main shutoff in the basement or front vault.

Plumbing questions in Washington, DC

Do you have plumbers in all of the District of Columbia?

Yes. PipeMend takes plumbing calls from every ward of the District, around the clock, and from nearby Maryland and Virginia suburbs.

Do plumbers need a license in the District of Columbia?

Yes. The DC Board of Industrial Trades (Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection) licenses plumbers in the District of Columbia. Gas line work may need its own license or endorsement.

Do I need a permit for plumbing work in the District of Columbia?

Often, yes. Most local building departments in the District of Columbia require permits for water heaters, gas lines, sewer and water service lines and repiping. Small repairs such as a faucet or toilet usually do not need one.

Does Washington, DC still have lead water lines?

Some homes still do, mostly older houses. DC Water runs a program to find and replace lead service lines, and you can check your address with the utility. A plumber can also look at the pipe where it enters your basement.
