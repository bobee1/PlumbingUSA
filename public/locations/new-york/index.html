What makes New York plumbing different

New York City and older upstate cities have huge numbers of prewar buildings with galvanized supply lines, cast-iron stacks and lead service lines. Steam heat, shared risers and tight access make repairs in older buildings slow.

Upstate, lake-effect snow and deep cold freeze pipes in Buffalo, Rochester and Syracuse. Heavy rain floods basements across the state. New York has no statewide plumber license; New York City licenses master plumbers, and other cities set their own rules.

Pipes, drains and housing age in New York

Old houses are common in New York: 30.3% date from before 1940. Original galvanized water pipes and cast-iron or clay drains are often near the end of their life. The EPA says homes built before 1986 are more likely to have lead pipes, fixtures and solder; a water test and your utility's service line records tell you for sure. See pipe repair and repiping, sewer line repair and water line repair.

Water heaters and gas lines in New York

Natural gas heats 59.8% of homes in New York, and gas homes usually have gas water heaters too. That means burners, venting and gas lines to keep safe. 14.6% of homes burn heating oil; many of them heat water with a boiler coil or an indirect tank. Most storage water heaters last 10 to 15 years, according to the Department of Energy. See water heater repair and tankless water heaters.

Storms, flooding and sewer backups in New York

Since 2000, FEMA has declared 42 water-related major disasters in New York, from floods, hurricanes and severe storms. The most recent was in 2024 (severe storm and flooding). Heavy rain overloads sewers and fills basements, so sump pumps, backwater valves and sewer line repairs matter here.

How to protect your plumbing in New York

  • Insulate pipes on outside walls and in basements.
  • Ask your utility about your service line material.
  • Test your sump pump before spring thaw.
  • Keep grease out of shared drain stacks.

Plumbing licenses and permits in New York

New York has no statewide plumber license. Cities and counties license plumbers and issue permits, so the rules in one town can differ from the next.

In New York, local building departments issue plumbing permits. Expect one for gas lines, water heaters, sewer or water service work and repiping.

Plumbing questions in New York

Do you have plumbers in all of New York?

Yes. PipeMend takes plumbing calls from all of New York, including New York City, Buffalo, Yonkers, Rochester and Syracuse, plus small towns and rural areas, 24 hours a day.

Do plumbers need a license in New York?

New York has no statewide plumber license, so cities and counties set the rules. Ask your local building department which license a plumber needs where you live.

Do I need a permit for plumbing work in New York?

Often, yes. Most local building departments in New York require permits for water heaters, gas lines, sewer and water service lines and repiping. Small repairs such as a faucet or toilet usually do not need one.

Do plumbers need a license in New York?

There is no statewide plumber license. New York City requires a licensed master plumber for most work, and many other cities and counties license plumbers. Check with your local building department.
