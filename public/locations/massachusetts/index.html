What makes Massachusetts plumbing different

Massachusetts has some of the oldest housing in the country. Triple-deckers and older homes in Boston, Worcester and Springfield often have galvanized supply lines, cast-iron stacks and lead or old service lines.

Cold winters freeze pipes on outside walls and in basements. Outside sewered areas, Title 5 rules require septic systems to be inspected when a home is sold, and failed systems must be upgraded. The Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters licenses plumbers.

Pipes, drains and housing age in Massachusetts

30.5% of homes in Massachusetts were built before 1940. Many still have galvanized steel supply pipes, cast-iron drains or clay sewer laterals, which rust, crack and let roots in. Homes built before 1986 are more likely to have lead pipes, fixtures or solder, according to the EPA. If yours is that old, test the water and ask your utility what your service line is made of. Water heaters and gas lines in Massachusetts

52% of homes here heat with natural gas, so gas water heaters and gas line work are a large share of plumbing calls. 20.2% of homes burn heating oil; many of them heat water with a boiler coil or an indirect tank. The Department of Energy says most tank water heaters last 10 to 15 years. See water heater repair and tankless water heaters.

Storms, flooding and sewer backups in Massachusetts

Massachusetts has had 19 water-related federal disaster declarations since 2000. The most recent was in 2026 (severe winter storm and snowstorm). Heavy rain overloads sewers and fills basements, so sump pumps, backwater valves and sewer line repairs matter here.

Practical plumbing tips for Massachusetts

  • Insulate basement and wall pipes before winter.
  • Ask your utility about your service line material.
  • Plan for Title 5 inspection before selling a home on septic.
  • Have stacks in older multi-family homes inspected.

Plumbing licenses and permits in Massachusetts

Plumbers in Massachusetts are licensed by the Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters. You can check a plumber's license there before work begins.

Most Massachusetts cities and counties require a permit for water heater replacement, gas line work, sewer and water line replacement and new piping. The plumber usually pulls it and schedules the inspection.

Plumbing questions in Massachusetts

Do you have plumbers in all of Massachusetts?

Yes. PipeMend takes plumbing calls from all of Massachusetts, including Boston, Worcester, Springfield, Cambridge and Lowell, plus small towns and rural areas, 24 hours a day.

Do plumbers need a license in Massachusetts?

Yes. The Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters licenses plumbers in Massachusetts. Gas line work may need its own license or endorsement.

Do I need a permit for plumbing work in Massachusetts?

Often, yes. Most local building departments in Massachusetts require permits for water heaters, gas lines, sewer and water service lines and repiping. Small repairs such as a faucet or toilet usually do not need one.

Who licenses plumbers in Massachusetts?

The Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters licenses plumbers and gas fitters. Local plumbing inspectors issue permits and inspect the work.
