Sump Pump Repair and Installation

A sump pump only matters when it rains hard, and that is when a failed pump floods a basement. PipeMend plumbers repair, replace and install sump pumps, with battery backup and alarms.

  • Battery backup systems

Quick answer: Sump pump service repairs or replaces the pump in the pit in your basement or crawl space. That pump pushes groundwater out and away from the house. A complete system has a correctly sized primary pump, a check valve, a properly routed discharge line, and ideally a battery backup pump and a high-water alarm. FEMA calls a battery backup an inexpensive safeguard against basement flooding.

How a sump pump system works

What sump pump service includes

  • Testing the pump, float switch, check valve and discharge line.
  • Repair of stuck floats, failed switches and leaking check valves.
  • Replacement with a pedestal or submersible pump sized to the inflow.
  • New installation: pit, liner, pump and discharge line.
  • Battery backup or water-powered backup pump installation.
  • High-water alarms that sound, or send a phone alert.
  • Discharge routing away from the foundation and to local rules.

The sump pump installation process

  1. Assess. The plumber looks at the pit, how fast water comes in, the discharge route and the outlet.
  2. Size the pump. Capacity is matched to the inflow and the height the water has to be lifted.
  3. Quote. You get a written estimate for the pump, backup, alarm and discharge work.
  4. Install. The pump is set level in the pit, with a check valve and a properly glued or clamped discharge.
  5. Route the discharge out of the house and away from the foundation.
  6. Test. The pit is filled with water to confirm both pumps start and stop, and the alarm sounds.

Why backup power matters

FEMA notes that a basement can flood when the primary pump stops working, the power goes out, or heavy rain overwhelms the pump. It describes a battery backup pump as an inexpensive safeguard, one that starts when water in the pit reaches its own float switch. FEMA also says sump pumps are used where basement flooding happens regularly, and where the water table sits above the foundation.

What a wet basement costs you

Water in a basement soaks drywall, carpet, stored items and furnaces. The EPA advises drying wet building materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. The Insurance Information Institute notes that sewer and drain backups are often left out of standard homeowners policies unless you add a backup rider, so check yours. A working pump, a backup and an alarm cost far less than a flooded basement.

What affects the cost of a sump pump

  • Repair, replacement or a new pit.
  • Pump type and capacity.
  • Battery or water-powered backup.
  • Discharge line length and routing.
  • Electrical work for a dedicated outlet.

DIY or call a professional?

You can test the pump with a bucket of water, clear debris from the pit and keep the outside discharge clear. Call a plumber for a pump that won't start, runs nonstop, short-cycles, or for a new pit, backup system or discharge reroute. Breaking into a basement floor for a new pit is a professional job.

Sump pump maintenance

  • Test the pump every few months and before storm season.
  • Keep the pit free of debris and the float free to move.
  • Replace backup batteries on the maker's schedule.
  • Make sure the outside discharge isn't frozen or buried.
  • Consider a backwater valve if the city sewer has backed up into your home.

Sump pump questions

How do I know if my sump pump is working?

Slowly pour a bucket of water into the pit. The float should rise, the pump should start, empty the pit and shut off. Check outside that water comes out of the discharge pipe. If it doesn't start, hums, or runs without lowering the water, call a plumber.

Why does my sump pump run all the time?

Common causes are a stuck float switch, a failed check valve that lets water fall back into the pit, a pump too small for the inflow, or a high water table after heavy rain.

Do I need a battery backup sump pump?

Storms that bring heavy rain often knock out power too. FEMA describes a battery backup pump as an inexpensive safeguard against basement flooding. It starts when water reaches its own float, even if the main pump or power fails.

How long does a sump pump last?

Many sump pumps last around 7 to 10 years, depending on how often they run and the pump quality. A pump that runs a lot, is noisy or is rusted should be replaced before the rainy season.

Where should the sump pump discharge go?

Away from the house, so the water doesn't soak back toward the foundation. Many cities do not allow sump pumps to drain into the sanitary sewer. A plumber routes the line to meet local rules.
