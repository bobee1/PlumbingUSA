What PipeMend does

Plumbing problems rarely wait for a good time. A supply line splits behind the washer, the kitchen drain backs up the night before a holiday, or the water heater gives out on a January morning. PipeMend exists so that one call to 1-800-555-0199 gets the right plumber moving, wherever the property is.

We take calls 24/7 from homeowners, renters, landlords, property managers and business owners in all 50 states and Washington, D.C. We have been in business for 10+ years. The work itself is done by licensed plumbers who cover your area: leak repair, drain cleaning, water heater repair and installation, sewer line work, gas lines, sump pumps and fixture repairs.

How a call to PipeMend works

  1. You call. Tell us the address and ZIP code, what is happening and when it started. Is water spreading? Is there a gas smell? Is the home occupied? Those answers set the priority.
  2. First steps on the phone. If water is leaking, we help you find and close the main shutoff valve. If you smell gas, we tell you to leave the building and call 911 and your gas company from outside.
  3. A plumber diagnoses the problem. Depending on the job, that can mean a drain camera, a pressure test, an acoustic leak check or a look at the water heater's burner and valves.
  4. You get a written estimate. The plumber explains the cause, the fix and any options, such as repair versus replacement, before work begins.
  5. The repair is made and tested. Water is turned back on, joints are checked for leaks, drains are run, and the work area is cleaned.

Why one national number helps

Searching for a plumber during an emergency means calling list after list of numbers and leaving voicemails. With PipeMend, the same number works in every state, at any hour. You describe the problem once, and the job goes to a plumber who covers your ZIP code. That matters most for landlords and property managers with homes in more than one city, and for anyone helping a parent or relative from far away.

How we work

  • Safety first. Gas smells, sewage and water near electrical outlets are treated as urgent, and you get clear safety steps before a plumber arrives.
  • Licensed for the job. Each repair goes to a plumber who holds the license your state and city require. Gas work goes to plumbers allowed to do gas work where you live.
  • Written estimates. You see the scope and price before work starts.
  • Code and permits. Work follows your local plumbing code, which is usually based on a model code such as the International Plumbing Code or the Uniform Plumbing Code. Permits are pulled when the job requires one.
  • Clear records. You get photos and notes on what was found and fixed, which helps with warranties, insurance claims and selling the home later.
